Verdict after 6 experiments

Partially supported

Cheap reverb-flavoured augmentation lifts validation macro-F1 0.612 → 0.671 (+5.9 pts) at negligible extra compute — but ≈4.5 of those 5.9 points land on common/mid species. The rare tail barely moves.

  • Not every "natural" augmentation helped. Pitch/time stretch regressed the model to 0.589 — below the no-aug baseline — by smearing rare-class cues. SNR-controlled noise mixing alone was a null result (+0.002, inside ±0.006 seed noise).
  • The best recipe is a stack, not a single trick: light IR reverb + noise mix + SpecAugment + a partial class-balanced sampler. Heavy reverb (long RT60) helped only common classes and came in below the stack (0.663).
  • Next move: class-conditional augmentation — apply each transform only where it helps and protect the rare tail; global augmentation has run out of road on the classes that need it most.

The bet

Small-data bioacoustics: a few thousand labelled clips, a long-tailed species list, one desktop GPU. Data is scarce and expensive (someone records and labels a Wood Thrush at dawn); compute is merely finite. So murmur holds the architecture fixed — a small CNN on log-mel spectrograms — and moves only the augmentation pipeline. This is deliberately not "does augmentation help" (it usually does); it's "do the natural-reverberation-flavoured, near-free ones help, and where do they help versus hurt."

H1 — efficacy partially supported

Reverb-family augmentation lifts validation macro-F1 over a no-augmentation baseline on a fixed small CNN.

Status: the lift is real (+5.9 pts) but concentrated in common/mid species — and two family members (pitch/stretch, heavy reverb) hurt or helped only the head.

H2 — efficiency asserted, not shown

The lift is competitive with, and cheaper than, swapping in a larger backbone — augmentation is the better marginal spend when data, not compute, binds.

Status: the cost side holds (augmentations add well under a minute per run), but the fair bigger/pretrained-backbone comparison has never been run. Planned as experiment 3 below.

The setup

6,200 call-centred clips (5 s, mono, 32 kHz), 34 species, log-mel features (128 mels, 25 ms / 10 ms). 70/15/15 stratified split; ~930 validation clips. The ~930-clip test split has not been touched during search — every number on this page is validation macro-F1. (Dataset is fictional but internally consistent, curated in the style of public field recordings.)

Why macro-F1, and why augmentation over architecture

Macro-F1 (F1 averaged per class, so a 41-clip species counts as much as a 480-clip one) weights the tail equally with the head. A model that only learns the common eight looks great on accuracy and mediocre on macro-F1 — that gap is the point.

Augmentation is near-free and physically motivated: field recordings already contain reverberation, wind, distance attenuation, overlapping calls; simulating those expands the data along axes the model meets at inference. Architecture swaps mostly buy capacity we can't feed — a bigger or pretrained backbone costs more per run, risks overfitting a 6k-clip set, and doesn't address the tail. Impulse-response convolution and SNR-controlled noise mixing (glossed below) are single fast ops.

Hardware: one RTX-class 16 GB GPU, 64 GB RAM. Runs so far: 41 min → 1 h 18 min.

Failed directions — the post-mortems

Two members of the "natural-variation" family did not survive contact with the tail. Both failures point the same way: augmentation strength should be conditioned on class, not global.

Pitch-shift / time-stretch hurt the rare classes exp-03 · 0.589

What happened: ±2-semitone pitch-shift + 0.9–1.1× time-stretch dropped macro-F1 below the no-aug baseline.

Why (best current understanding): many deep-tail species are separated by fine formant/harmonic structure and call timing. Warping pitch and tempo pushes synthetic examples across the decision boundaries the model needs to keep sharp — and with <50 real clips there aren't enough true examples to anchor against the corrupted ones. Common classes have the data to shrug it off, so the aggregate hides an asymmetric harm.

Honest caveat: only one strength was tested (±2 st). A gentler ±0.5 st might be neutral rather than harmful — untested. The claim is "at this strength it hurt the tail," not "pitch aug is useless."

Disposition: dropped. Candidate to revisit only as a class-conditional transform, never applied to deep-tail classes.

Heavy reverb helped common classes only exp-06 · 0.663

What happened: long-RT60 (0.8–1.5 s), high-probability reverb landed −0.008 below the light-reverb stack.

Why: long reverb tails wash short, low-energy rare-class calls into the spectrogram's noise floor, while long, loud common-class songs stay legible and even benefit from the added room variation. Net: common up, rare down, small aggregate loss.

Honest caveat: this is a per-tier decomposition on validation, not a controlled per-class ablation — the mechanism is inferred, not proven.

Disposition: keep reverb light. Reinforces the class-conditional thesis.

Current best recipe

Reproduces exp-05 (val macro-F1 0.671). Nothing here is expensive — the augmentations add well under a minute per run over the clean baseline. That's the H2 cost story holding up; the H2 comparison still isn't.

backbone: small_cnn            # unchanged control across all experiments
input: logmel_128              # 128 mels, 25ms/10ms
train: {lr: 3e-4, batch: 32, epochs: 60, schedule: cosine}
sampler:
  class_balanced: 0.5          # partial reweight toward the tail
aug:
  reverb_ir:  {p: 0.5, rt60_range: [0.3, 0.6]}   # light, measured IRs
  noise_mix:  {snr_db: [5, 20], beds: [wind, rain, chorus]}
  specaugment: {freq_masks: 2, time_masks: 2}
  # pitch_shift / time_stretch: OFF (regressed, see exp-03)
  # heavy reverb: OFF (common-only, see exp-06)

Open questions the ledger can't answer yet

  • Is the tail even learnable from 41 clips? The ceiling is unknown — needs a small oracle study varying clips-per-class to separate "augmentation can't help the tail" from "nothing can, at this n."
  • Does noise-mix only help through SpecAugment? exp-04 vs exp-05 suggests composition, but it's one comparison — wants a proper 2×2 ablation.
  • How much of exp-05's gain is the balanced sampler vs the augmentations? Currently confounded — both were introduced in the same run.
  • Does H2 survive a fair backbone baseline? The bigger/pretrained arm has never been run; until then H2 is asserted, not shown.
  • Do measured IRs generalize? All impulse responses come from a small room library; field conditions may differ.

The one-paragraph verdict

murmur's core bet is partially supported. Cheap reverb-flavoured augmentation does lift a small bird-call classifier — +5.9 validation macro-F1 points, 0.612 → 0.671, at negligible extra compute — but the gain is concentrated in the common and mid classes and barely reaches the rare tail, and not every member of the family helps (pitch/stretch regressed; heavy reverb helped only the head). The efficiency half of the hypothesis (H2) is still asserted, not shown, because the fair bigger-backbone comparison hasn't been run. The clear next step is to stop augmenting globally and start augmenting per class — protect the tail from the transforms that smear it while keeping the head's gains.
