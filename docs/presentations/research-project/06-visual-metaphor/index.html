murmur · a small-data bioacoustics project

An expedition up the macro-F1 slope

Teaching a small classifier to hear a single bird through cheap, physically-plausible noise. The bet — that near-free reverb-flavoured augmentations beat reaching for a bigger model — partially works: the route climbed +5.9 points, but the summit that matters is still in cloud.

The wager: ultra-cheap augmentations inspired by natural reverberation lift validation macro-F1 on a small, long-tailed dataset (6,200 clips, 34 species) more per unit of compute than a bigger or pretrained backbone would. Architecture is held fixed (a small CNN on log-mel spectrograms) — only the augmentation pipeline moves.

How to read this map

Height on the map= validation macro-F1 (the score; higher = better). It weights the rare tail equally with the common head.
Solid trail= the recipe lineage that was kept: 1 → 2 → 4 → 5.
Camp (numbered)= one experiment, placed at the score it reached.
Dashed downhill spur= a direction explored and abandoned (it lost height).
High Camp (gold)= the current best recipe (exp-05, 0.671).
Dashed uphill routes= the three planned next experiments, not yet walked.
Clouded summit= the rare-tail ceiling — how high the deep-tail classes can actually go is unknown.
▲▲ Faded ridges behind= scenery only, no meaning. Only the labelled trail and axis carry data.

Reading the map in one glance

Six experiments, March–June 2026, same seed and split and backbone unless noted. The trail climbs when an idea helps and drops when it hurts. It rose quickly off the trailhead, walked a nearly flat stretch, then reached its highest point at Camp 5. Two side-trips led downhill and were abandoned. The summit — good scores on the rarest species — stays in cloud. Note the scale of the whole picture: the entire climb spans just 0.082 macro-F1, and seed-to-seed wobble on this setup is ≈ ±0.006, so small steps deserve suspicion.

Trailhead · the floor exp-01

Small CNN on log-mel spectrograms, no augmentation at all. LR 3e-4, batch 32, 60 epochs, cosine decay. 41 min.

Establishes where the climb starts: 0.612. Trains cleanly but overfits by about epoch 35; the common head is already near-saturated while the tail classes carry almost all the error. The lesson that shapes everything after: the problem is generalization on scarce classes, not model capacity.

Camp 2 · the first ascent exp-02

Add light impulse-response reverb (short measured rooms, RT60 ≈ 0.3–0.6 s), p=0.5. 47 min.

The first clear win: 0.641 (+0.029). The gain is broad but skews to the mid and common tiers; the deep tail barely moves. Encouraging enough to make light reverb the spine of every recipe that follows.

The ravine · turned back exp-03

On top of Camp 2, add pitch-shift (±2 semitones) and time-stretch (0.9–1.1×). 58 min.

A regression, all the way down to 0.589 — below the no-aug baseline. Warping pitch and tempo smears the fine formant and timing cues the rare classes depend on, pushing synthetic examples across the very decision boundaries the model needs to keep sharp; several deep-tail species collapse toward zero recall. Common classes shrug it off, hiding the asymmetric harm in the average. Honest caveat: only one strength (±2 st) was tested — a gentler ±0.5 st might be neutral. The claim is "at this strength it hurt the tail," not "pitch aug is useless." Dropped from the pipeline.

Camp 4 · the flat stretch exp-04

Drop pitch/stretch; add SNR-controlled background-noise mixing (wind, rain, chorus) at 5–20 dB, on top of Camp 2. 52 min.

Lands at 0.643 — just +0.002 over Camp 2, inside run-to-run noise. A null result: noise mixing neither helps nor hurts on its own at this scale. Kept in the pack anyway on the hunch that it would compose with SpecAugment further up — a hunch Camp 5 vindicates.

The spur · a step back down exp-06

From High Camp, push reverb hard — long RT60 (0.8–1.5 s), p=0.7, replacing the light setting. 1 h 18 min.

Lands at 0.663 — below the 0.671 of the light-reverb stack. Long reverb tails wash short, quiet rare-class calls into the spectrogram's noise floor, while long, loud common-class songs stay legible and even benefit. Common up, rare down, small net loss. Caveat: this is a per-tier read on validation, not a controlled per-class ablation — the mechanism is inferred. Keep reverb light.

High Camp · the current best recipe exp-05 · 0.671

The full stack: light reverb + noise mixing + SpecAugment (2 freq / 2 time masks) + a mildly class-balanced sampler (0.5). 1 h 12 min.

The highest point reached: 0.671, +0.059 over the trailhead. The components compose — noise mixing that was null alone becomes useful once SpecAugment is present, and the partial balanced sampler recovers a little tail recall. But a decomposition shows ~4.5 of those 5.9 points come from the common and mid tiers. The view from up here is the real story: the climb lifted the well-trodden lower slopes, and the summit ridge barely moved.

The clouded summit · what we can't yet see

Height still to be gained sits mostly on the rarest species, and it's honestly unknown how much is reachable. The open questions the map can't answer:

  • Is the tail even learnable from 41 clips? The ceiling is unknown — it needs a small oracle study varying clips-per-class to separate "augmentation can't help the tail" from "nothing can, at this n."
  • Does noise-mix only help through SpecAugment? exp-04 vs exp-05 hints at composition, but that's one comparison; it wants a proper 2×2 ablation.
  • How much of Camp 5's gain is the balanced sampler vs the augmentations? Confounded — the sampler arrived in the same run.
  • Does the efficiency claim (H2) survive a fair backbone baseline? The "bigger / pretrained backbone" arm has never actually been run. Until it is, H2 is asserted, not shown.
  • Do measured IRs generalize to unseen environments? All impulse responses come from one small room library; field conditions may differ.

Routes planned from High Camp

The three next experiments — the dashed uphill lines on the map.

  1. class-conditional-aug — stop augmenting globally. Apply each transform only in the tiers where the ledger shows it helps: light reverb everywhere, but pitch/stretch and heavy reverb excluded from the rare and deep-tail classes. The direct test of the "condition on class" thesis. Est. ~1 h.
  2. sampler-vs-aug-ablation — a clean 2×2, (balanced sampler on/off) × (aug stack on/off), to de-confound Camp 5. ~4 runs × ~50 min.
  3. backbone-fairness-arm — finally run the architecture control: a larger / AudioSet-pretrained backbone with no augmentation, matched compute, to see whether the +5.9 augmentation points beat what a bigger model buys. Settles H2. The heaviest run on the docket, est. up to ~6 h, overnight in a worktree.

Where this map lies

A mountain has one elevation, but macro-F1 is an average over 34 very differently-placed species. Drawing the route as a single line hides that the common head is already far up the slope while the rare tail never left base camp — the same "height" is stitched from wildly unequal climbs. And on a real mountain higher is simply harder; here the relationship between augmentation strength and score is non-monotone and class-dependent (more reverb helped the head and hurt the tail). Treat the trail as a story of one number over time, not as terrain you could actually stand on.

The verdict. murmur's core bet is partially supported. Cheap reverb-flavoured augmentation does lift a small bird-call classifier — +5.9 validation macro-F1 points, 0.612 → 0.671, at negligible extra compute — but the gain is concentrated in the common and mid classes and barely reaches the rare tail, and not every member of the family helps (pitch/stretch regressed, heavy reverb helped only the head). The efficiency half of the hypothesis (H2) is still asserted, not shown, because the fair bigger-backbone comparison hasn't been run. The clear next step is to stop augmenting globally and start augmenting per class — protect the tail from the transforms that smear it while keeping the head's gains. All figures are validation macro-F1; the ~930-clip test split has not been touched during search.
