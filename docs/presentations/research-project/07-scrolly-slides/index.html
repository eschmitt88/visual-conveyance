2 · The bet

Data is scarce; compute is merely finite. Which do you spend on?

Do ultra-cheap, natural-reverb-flavoured augmentations lift a small classifier more per unit of compute than reaching for a bigger model would?

The architecture is the control — a small CNN on log-mel spectrograms, held fixed. Only the augmentation pipeline moves.

3 · The data, and the difficulty

A small, heavily long-tailed dataset

6,200call-centred clips
34species (classes)
5species under 45 clips

The top class has 480 clips; the deep tail sits under 45. The primary metric is validation macro-F1 — it weights the rare tail equally with the common head. That gap is the point.

4 · Six experiments, one metric

The ledger, as a trajectory

baseline win regression null best common-only

Validation macro-F1 across the ledger. One dip below baseline, one flat step, and a peak at exp-05. Seed-to-seed variability is ≈ ±0.006.

5 · exp-02 · reverb-light-ir

First win: light reverb

0.641+0.029 vs baseline

Convolve waveforms with short measured room impulse responses (RT60 ≈ 0.3–0.6 s). The gain is broad but skews to common and mid tiers — the deep tail barely moves. Enough to make reverb the spine of the pipeline.

6 · exp-03 · pitchshift-timestretch

The regression: pitch & time stretch

0.589−0.023 below the no-aug baseline

±2-semitone pitch-shift and 0.9–1.1× time-stretch smear the fine formant and timing cues rare classes depend on — several deep-tail species collapse toward zero recall. Common classes are unbothered, which hides the asymmetric harm.

7 · exp-04 · noise-mix-snr

The null result: noise mixing

+0.002vs reverb-only — inside run-to-run noise (±0.006)

SNR-controlled background beds (wind, rain, distant chorus) at 5–20 dB. On its own, neither helps nor hurts at this scale. Kept in the stack only on the hypothesis that it composes with SpecAugment.

8 · exp-05 · stack-reverb-noise-specaug

The best recipe is a stack, not a single trick

0.671+0.059 vs baseline · current best

Light reverb + noise mixing + SpecAugment + a partial class-balanced sampler. The components compose: noise mixing that was null alone becomes useful once SpecAugment is present. Yet ~4.5 of the 5.9 points still come from common and mid tiers.

9 · exp-06 · reverb-heavy-longrt60

More reverb isn't more gain

0.663−0.008 vs the light-reverb stack

Long-RT60, high-probability reverb helps common classes further — they have the data to absorb it — but washes short, quiet rare calls into the noise floor. The effect is class-dependent, not monotone in reverb strength.

10 · Per-species F1: baseline vs best

The shape of the table is the result

exp-01 baseline F1 exp-05 best F1

6–7 points added to the head, a few to the mid, almost nothing to the rare tail — and a slight regression on the single deepest class. Global augmentation has run out of road where it's needed most.

11 · Honest verdict

Partially supported — not cleanly confirmed.

Cheap reverb augmentation does lift the classifier at negligible extra compute. But the gain skews to common classes, and the efficiency half of the bet (H2) is still asserted, not shown: the fair bigger-backbone arm has never been run.

12 · Recap & what's next

The takeaways

  • Reverb augmentation lifts macro-F1 0.612 → 0.671 (+5.9) — but mostly on common species; the rare tail barely moves.
  • Not all of the family helps: pitch/time-stretch regressed to 0.589; noise-mix alone was a null (+0.002).
  • The best result is a stack — light reverb + noise + SpecAugment — not any single augmentation.
  • Heavy reverb helped the head only; the effect is class-dependent, not monotone.
  • Next move: class-conditional augmentation — apply each transform only where the ledger shows it helps.
