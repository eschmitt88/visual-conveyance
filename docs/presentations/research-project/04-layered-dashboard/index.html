🎯The bet

Cheap, natural-reverb-inspired audio augmentations beat reaching for a bigger model when the binding constraint is data, not compute.

Architecture is held fixed — a small CNN on log-mel spectrograms — so the only thing that moves is the augmentation pipeline.

▶

The hypothesis, in the project's own words:

"Ultra-cheap audio augmentations inspired by natural reverberation improve bird-call classification on small datasets — more per unit of compute than reaching for a bigger or pretrained architecture would."

Two sub-claims, tested separately

  • H1 — efficacy. Reverb-family augmentation lifts validation macro-F1 over a no-augmentation baseline on a fixed small CNN.
  • H2 — efficiency. That lift is competitive with, and cheaper than, the lift from swapping in a larger backbone — augmentation is the better marginal spend when data, not compute, is scarce.

The bet is deliberately narrow: not "does augmentation help" (it usually does) but "do the natural-reverberation-flavoured, near-free ones help, and where do they help versus hurt?" Field recordings already contain reverb, wind, distance and overlap, so simulating those cheaply expands the data along axes the model actually meets at inference.

⚠H2 is asserted, not shown. The fair "bigger / pretrained backbone" comparison has never been run. Until it is, the efficiency half of the bet is a claim, not a result.

🐦The dataset — long-tailed by design

6,200 clips, 34 species, heavily long-tailed. The tail is the whole difficulty: 11 species have <80 clips, 5 have <45.

The 8 most common species are ~52% of all clips (top class, American Robin, has 480). Median is ≈120 clips per class.

exp-02 — light impulse-response reverb (RT60 ≈ 0.3–0.6 s, p=0.5). First clear win, broad but skewed to mid/common tiers. Made reverb the spine.

exp-04 — SNR-controlled background-noise mixing (5–20 dB; wind, rain, chorus) on top of reverb. +0.002 over exp-02 — inside noise, a null on its own; kept only on the bet it would compose with SpecAugment.

exp-06 — pushed reverb hard (long RT60 0.8–1.5 s, p=0.7). Landed at 0.663, below the light stack: heavy reverb helps common classes but blurs short, quiet rare-class calls.

Seed-to-seed variability is ≈ ±0.006 macro-F1 — which is why exp-04's +0.002 over exp-02 is read as null, not a win.

✅What worked — a stack, not a hero augmentation

The best recipe is a stack: light reverb + noise mixing + SpecAugment + a partial class-balanced sampler. The components compose.

0.671 val macro-F1 · exp-05
▶

Noise mixing that was null on its own (exp-04) becomes useful once SpecAugment is present, and the partial balanced sampler recovers a little tail recall. No single augmentation gets you here.

backbone: small_cnn            # control across all runs
input: logmel_128              # 128 mels, 25ms/10ms
train: {lr: 3e-4, batch: 32, epochs: 60, schedule: cosine}
sampler:
  class_balanced: 0.5          # partial reweight toward the tail
aug:
  reverb_ir:  {p: 0.5, rt60_range: [0.3, 0.6]}   # light, measured IRs
  noise_mix:  {snr_db: [5, 20], beds: [wind, rain, chorus]}
  specaugment: {freq_masks: 2, time_masks: 2}
  # pitch_shift / time_stretch: OFF (regressed)
  # heavy reverb: OFF (common-only)

Nothing here is expensive — the augmentations add well under a minute of per-run wall-clock over the clean baseline. That is the efficiency story holding up: the win came from data-space moves, not compute.

⚠The gain is uneven. Of the 5.9 points, ≈4.5 come from the common/mid tiers. And the balanced sampler was introduced in the same run as the augmentations, so its share of the gain versus the augmentations is currently confounded.

🧪What didn't — the honest failures

Not every "reverb-inspired" transform helped. Pitch/time-stretch actively regressed the model to 0.589; noise-mix alone was a null (+0.002).

pitch/stretch −0.052 vs exp-02 noise-mix +0.002 (inside noise)

▶

Pitch-shift / time-stretch hurt the rare classes (exp-03)

Adding ±2-semitone pitch-shift and 0.9–1.1× time-stretch dropped macro-F1 to 0.589 — below the no-aug baseline. Many deep-tail species are separated by fine formant/harmonic structure and call timing; warping pitch and tempo moves synthetic examples across the exact decision boundaries the model needs kept sharp, and with <50 real clips there aren't enough true examples to anchor against the corrupted ones. Common classes shrug it off, so the aggregate hides an asymmetric harm.

⚠Only one strength was tested (±2 st). A gentler ±0.5 st might be neutral rather than harmful — untested. The claim is "at the strength we tried, it hurt the tail," not "pitch aug is useless."

Noise mixing alone was a null (exp-04)

+0.002 over reverb-only sits inside the ±0.006 seed spread — it neither helps nor hurts on its own at this scale. Kept in the stack only on the (later vindicated) bet that it composes with SpecAugment.

Heavy reverb helped common classes only (exp-06)

Long-RT60, high-probability reverb landed at 0.663, below the light stack's 0.671. Long reverb tails wash out short, low-energy rare-class calls while long, loud common-class songs stay legible and even benefit — common up, rare down, small aggregate loss.

⚠The per-tier split is a decomposition on validation, not a controlled per-class ablation — the mechanism is inferred, not proven.

⚖️The honest verdict

Core bet: partially supported. Cheap reverb-flavoured augmentation does lift the classifier (+5.9 pts at negligible compute) — but the gain is concentrated in common/mid classes and barely reaches the rare tail, and not every family member helps.

▶

Baseline 0.612 → best 0.671, +5.9 validation macro-F1 points, at well under a minute of added per-run wall-clock. Pitch/stretch regressed; heavy reverb helped only the head. The efficiency half of the hypothesis (H2) is still asserted, not shown, because the fair bigger-backbone comparison hasn't been run.

⚠All numbers here are validation macro-F1. The ~930-clip test split has not been touched during the search — a single held-out scoring pass runs only after the best recipe is frozen.

The clear next step is to stop augmenting globally and start augmenting per class: protect the tail from the transforms that smear it while keeping the head's gains.

🧭What's next

The clear next move is class-conditional augmentation: apply each transform only where the ledger shows it helps, and protect the rare tail — global augmentation has plateaued on the common classes.

▶

Next three planned experiments

  • class-conditional-aug — light reverb everywhere, but pitch/stretch and heavy reverb excluded from rare/deep-tail classes. Direct test of the "condition on class" thesis. Est. ~1 h.
  • sampler-vs-aug-ablation — a clean 2×2 (balanced sampler on/off) × (aug stack on/off) to de-confound exp-05's gain. ~4 runs × ~50 min.
  • backbone-fairness-arm — finally run the architecture control: a larger / AudioSet-pretrained backbone with no augmentation, matched compute, to settle H2. Est. up to ~6 h (heaviest run, overnight in a worktree).

Open questions the plan has to face

  • Is the tail even learnable from 41 clips? The ceiling is unknown — wants a small oracle study varying clips-per-class.
  • Does noise-mix only help through SpecAugment? One comparison suggests composition; wants a proper 2×2.
  • How much of exp-05's gain is the balanced sampler vs the augmentations? Currently confounded.
  • Do measured IRs generalize to unseen recording environments? All IRs come from a small room library.

Compute envelope

Single RTX-class desktop GPU (16 GB), 64 GB RAM — no cluster. Runs so far span 41 min → 1 h 18 min; the pretrained-backbone arm is the outlier at an estimated up to ~6 h. Data footprint (~6,200 clips) is small.
