Primary result — overall verdict: “can’t tell”

Trial-to-paid conversion moved 11.4% → 12.2% (+0.8pp), CI [−0.3, +1.9], p=0.16 — not statistically significant. Don’t quote it as a win, and don’t quote it as “no effect” either: the CI includes both −0.3pp and +1.9pp.

Verdict: inconclusive overall · p = 0.16

Difference (B − A): +0.8pp, 95% CI [−0.3, +1.9], p = 0.16 (two-proportion z-test, two-sided — a standard test of whether two conversion rates differ).

Why “can’t tell” is the honest reading. The CI includes zero and includes effects as negative as −0.3pp, so the data can’t rule out “no benefit.” It equally includes +1.9pp, so it can’t rule out a real gain. The blended effect is too small relative to noise — and too internally heterogeneous (see the platform split) — to support a global ship decision either way.

Caveat — power The experiment had 80% power to detect an overall lift of +1.55pp; it was not powered to confirm effects smaller than ~1.5pp overall. A +0.8pp point estimate sits below what this test could reliably detect.

The blend hides the story. The overall number is not “a modest win everywhere” — it is a strong mobile-web win and a real iOS loss averaging each other out. Weighted by trial-start counts, the platform effects reconcile to the blended result: (2,354 × 4.2 − 2,535 × 1.9 + 1,736 × 0.1) / 6,625 ≈ +0.8pp.

Platform split — the real story (pre-registered)

Mobile-web improved +4.2pp (CI [+2.1, +6.3], p<0.0001); iOS regressed −1.9pp (CI [−3.6, −0.2], p=0.027); Android was null. Platform was the single subgroup chosen before launch — this is not a cut found by fishing.

How seriously to take the subgroups

Caveat — multiple comparisons Three platform tests were run. Under a Bonferroni-adjusted threshold (α = 0.0167 — a stricter bar that compensates for testing three subgroups), mobile-web (p<0.0001) survives easily; iOS (p=0.027) does not survive the strict correction.

Cutting the other way: platform was the single pre-registered subgroup dimension (analysis plan v1.2, filed 2026-04-28, before launch — i.e., chosen in advance, not discovered by scanning many cuts), motivated by a stated mechanism: the three platforms have different payment surfaces (web checkout vs App Store vs Play Store). Pre-registration plus a coherent mechanism makes the iOS regression a credible signal that warrants action — a targeted follow-up experiment — while stopping short of proof. The mobile-web result needs no such hedging.

Why iOS regressed — the step-3 permission prompt

Pathways moved the notification-permission ask from a day-2 soft card to onboarding step 3, where iOS fires the system dialog — and iOS step-3 drop-off jumped 8.1% → 12.7%. No comparable shift on mobile-web or Android. That concentration is why the call is “fix and re-test,” not “abandon iOS.”

Caveat — not causal on its own The dismissed-vs-accepted comparison is confounded by user intent (people who accept prompts differ from people who dismiss them) and is not causal evidence by itself. The funnel data is exploratory, not pre-registered.

Why “fix and re-test.” The regression concentrates at exactly the changed step, on exactly the platform with the highest-friction system dialog. That is why the recommendation is a targeted iOS fix experiment rather than abandoning iOS.

Design & validity — checks passed, one caveat on power

184,204 signups randomized 50/50; no sample-ratio mismatch; instrumentation audited clean; the trial-starter denominator is sound. The test was powered for a +1.55pp overall lift — effects smaller than ~1.5pp were never confirmable overall. One minor deviation from the analysis plan.

184,204 randomized · SRM χ² p=0.96 · powered for +1.55pp

What was tested

The current flow (A) has 9 screens with the notification-permission prompt deferred to day 2. Pathways (B) shortens the flow to 6 screens, adds a goal-setting quiz that personalizes the starter course, moves the notification-permission ask to step 3, and adds a social-proof screen. Hypothesis: Pathways increases trial-to-paid conversion by making the first-session experience faster and more personally relevant.

Primary metric

Trial-to-paid conversion: among users who start a free trial, the share whose first payment succeeds within 14 days of trial start. Denominator = trial starts, not all randomized users — see “denominator integrity” below for why that’s safe here.

Sample, power, duration

  • Randomized: 184,204 new signups, split 50/50 at account creation (A: 92,114; B: 92,090). Assignment sticky per account; no crossover observed.
  • Analyzed for the primary metric (trial starters): 13,249 (A: 6,602; B: 6,647).
  • Power: assuming an 11.5% baseline and ~6,400 trial starters per arm, 80% power to detect an absolute lift of +1.55pp at two-sided α = 0.05. Not powered to confirm effects smaller than ~1.5pp overall — key context for the +0.8pp point estimate.
  • Duration: six weeks of enrollment (2026-05-04 → 2026-06-14), fixed in advance; conversion window closed 2026-06-28. No peeking-based early stop; interim looks were monitoring-only (guardrails and SRM).
  • Pre-registered subgroups: exactly one dimension — platform (mobile-web / iOS / Android) — in analysis plan v1.2, filed 2026-04-28 before launch, motivated by the differing payment surfaces. Any other cut in the readout is labeled exploratory.

Diagnostics

  • Sample-ratio mismatch (SRM — a check that the 50/50 split actually landed 50/50): A: 92,114 vs B: 92,090; χ² p = 0.96. No SRM; weekly monitoring never alarmed.
  • Instrumentation: assignment and exposure logging verified against the payments ledger on a 2% audit sample (n = 3,684): zero cross-arm contamination, zero duplicate assignment, conversions reconciled within 0.1%.
  • Denominator integrity: because the metric conditions on trial start, a treatment that changed who starts trials would bias the comparison. Trial-start rate is statistically flat (7.17% vs 7.22%, p = 0.69) and platform mix among trialists is near-identical across arms (within 0.4pp per platform), so the conditioned comparison is sound.
Deviation from plan (one) A planned supplementary Bayesian shrinkage estimate of weekly deltas was dropped for time, replaced by the pooled late-window checks (weeks 4–6). No registered primary or subgroup analysis was altered.

Recommendation — narrow ship, targeted fix

Ship Pathways to mobile-web only (with a 5% holdback); do not ship iOS or Android; run an iOS fix experiment on the permission prompt. Not a full rollout, not a kill — and definitely not “B won overall.”

Next steps

  1. Ship Pathways to mobile-web only, target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback (a slice kept on the old flow) for 8 weeks — to confirm the +4.2pp effect out-of-experiment and monitor for post-novelty drift.
  2. Do not ship iOS. Launch a follow-up experiment (ONB-2026-019, draft) testing Pathways-on-iOS with the permission prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus a second arm with a pre-permission explainer screen. Primary metric unchanged; powered for a ±1.5pp iOS-only effect; est. 8 weeks.
  3. Do not ship Android. Null result; revisit after the iOS fix experiment settles the prompt question, since Android shares the flow.
  4. Retire the week-2 interim readout from internal decks; this readout supersedes it.
  5. Re-estimate revenue impact for the mobile-web ship using the late-window +3.8pp (conservative) to full-run +4.2pp range.

What this readout does NOT support

Guardrails on interpretation
  • Not “B increased conversion by 0.8 points” — the blended effect is not significant, and the blend averages a win with a loss.
  • Not a platform-wide ship — iOS would likely pay a real conversion cost (best estimate −1.9pp on the highest-revenue platform).
  • Not “the redesign failed” — mobile-web is a large, durable, pre-registered, mechanism-consistent win.
  • Not the early-week numbers — the +2.9pp week-1 snapshot reflects novelty that fully decayed and should not be cited.
