tessel onboarding A/B (Pathways): not a win — a split

Overall +0.8pp is not statistically significant; the pre-registered platform split — a real mobile-web win, a real iOS loss — is the story, and it points to a narrow ship, not a rollout and not a kill.

Overall trial-to-paid conversion moved 11.4% → 12.2% (+0.8pp, 95% CI [−0.3, +1.9], p = 0.16) — not statistically significant, so the honest blended verdict is “can’t tell,” not “B won.” The blend averages a strong mobile-web win against a real iOS loss, and the guardrail (7-day retention, all 184,204 randomized users) was flat at +0.2pp (CI [−0.2, +0.6]).

Platform was the single pre-registered subgroup: mobile-web +4.2pp (13.9% → 18.1%, CI [+2.1, +6.3], p < 0.0001), iOS −1.9pp (11.6% → 9.7%, CI [−3.6, −0.2], p = 0.027), Android +0.1pp (7.8% → 7.9%, CI [−1.7, +1.9], p = 0.92) — solid boxes have CIs excluding zero; dashed boxes straddle it. The iOS result does not survive a strict Bonferroni correction (α = 0.0167), but pre-registration plus a coherent mechanism makes it a credible signal that warrants action, short of proof; mobile-web needs no such hedging.

The overall delta decayed from +2.9pp (week 1) to +0.1pp (week 6) — consistent with a novelty effect — so the week-2 interim readout (+2.2pp, nominally significant at the time) is superseded and should not be cited; weekly CIs are wide (≈ ±2.7pp), so the pattern, not any single cell, is the signal. The platform split is not novelty: pooled weeks 4–6 (exploratory) still show mobile-web +3.8pp (CI ≈ [+0.8, +6.8]) while iOS stays negative at −2.2pp (CI ≈ [−4.6, +0.2], no longer individually significant at the halved sample).

Why iOS regressed: the step-3 permission prompt

Pathways moved the notification-permission ask from a day-2 soft card to onboarding step 3, and iOS abandonment at that step jumped 8.1% → 12.7% while mobile-web and Android barely moved — the regression concentrates exactly at the changed step, on the platform with the highest-friction system dialog. This funnel evidence is exploratory, not causal proof (B-arm iOS users who dismissed the dialog converted at 7.9% vs 10.4% for accepters, a comparison confounded by user intent), hence “fix and re-test iOS,” not “abandon iOS.”

Ship Pathways to mobile-web only (target 2026-07-06) with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks; do not ship iOS — instead run ONB-2026-019 testing the permission prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask (plus a pre-permission explainer arm, powered for ±1.5pp, est. 8 weeks) — and hold Android (null) until that settles, since it shares the flow. Revenue estimates for the mobile-web ship should use the conservative late-window +3.8pp to full-run +4.2pp range.
