2 / 12 · context

What was tested

A · current flow

  • 9 screens
  • Notification ask deferred to day 2 (soft in-product card)

B · “Pathways” redesign

  • 6 screens + goal-setting quiz (personalized starter course)
  • Notification ask moved to step 3
  • Social-proof screen added

184,204 new signups, split 50/50 for six weeks (May 4 – Jun 14, 2026). Primary metric: trial-to-paid conversion among the 13,249 who started a trial — first payment within 14 days.

3 / 12 · the headline number

The overall result is a “can’t tell”

+0.8pp  (11.4% → 12.2%)

95% CI [−0.3, +1.9] · p = 0.16 — not statistically significant

The interval crosses zero — a small loss and a ~2pp gain are both consistent with this data.

Do not read this as “B won” — and not as “no effect” either. The test had 80% power only for lifts of +1.55pp or more, so a +0.8pp true effect was never confirmable here.

4 / 12 · the real story

A pre-registered platform split: up on mobile-web, down on iOS

CI clears zero — win CI clears zero — loss CI straddles zero — null

Platform was the only subgroup pre-registered before launch, chosen for its differing payment surfaces. Mobile-web: a large, unambiguous win. iOS: a significant regression. Android: no evidence either way.

5 / 12 · why the headline looks like a weak win

Two opposite effects cancel in the blended number

+4.2pp × mobile-web  +  −1.9pp × iOS  +  +0.1pp × Android
≈ +0.8pp overall

weighted by each platform’s share of trial starts — the subgroup effects reconcile exactly with the blended result

+0.8pp is not “a modest win everywhere.” It is a strong win and a real loss averaging each other out — which is why no single blended number can carry this decision.

6 / 12 · when you look changes what you see

Early snapshots overstated B

Every week sits inside the noise zone — the decaying pattern is the signal, not any single bar.

The blended effect decayed from +2.9pp (week 1) to +0.1pp (week 6) — a novelty pattern. A week-2 interim readout (+2.2pp, nominally significant then) circulated internally; it is superseded and would have materially overstated B.

7 / 12 · the split survives the decay

The mobile-web gain is not just novelty

Weeks 4–6 only, pooled (exploratory; CIs ≈ [+0.8, +6.8] and [−4.6, +0.2]) — gray = interval now straddles zero at this halved sample.

After the novelty washed out, mobile-web still shows +3.8pp. iOS stays negative at −2.2pp and does not recover — though it is no longer individually significant on half the data.

8 / 12 · where the iOS loss happens

The likely culprit: the step-3 permission prompt

iOS 8.1%→12.7% drop-off at step 3, A → B
Mobile-web 3.2%→3.4% soft email opt-in, no OS dialog
Android 5.0%→5.6% lower-friction OS dialog

Share of users abandoning onboarding at step 3, of those reaching it (funnel data — exploratory, not pre-registered).

Pathways moved the notification ask from a day-2 soft card to onboarding step 3 — where iOS fires the system permission dialog. Drop-off jumped exactly there, on exactly the highest-friction platform, and barely moved elsewhere.

9 / 12 · honesty check

How much to trust the iOS signal

Against

Three platform tests were run. Under a Bonferroni-corrected threshold (α = 0.0167), iOS’s p = 0.027 does not survive.

For

Platform was the single pre-registered subgroup, chosen in advance for a stated mechanism (payment surfaces) — and the funnel points at the changed step.

Verdict: a credible signal that warrants action — a targeted fix experiment — while stopping short of proof. Mobile-web (p < 0.0001) survives the correction easily and needs no such hedging.

10 / 12 · guardrails

The guardrail held: retention is flat

+0.2pp

7-day retention, 31.2% → 31.4% · 95% CI [−0.2, +0.6] — tight around zero: Pathways neither helps nor harms early return

Measured on all 184k randomized users, not just trialists — the conversion story comes at no retention cost (and brings no retention gain). Support tickets blipped +12% in weeks 1–2, then normalized after a copy fix.

11 / 12 · recommendation

Narrow ship — not a rollout, not a kill

  • ✓Mobile-web — ship. Target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks to confirm the effect out-of-experiment.
  • ✗iOS — do not ship. Run fix experiment ONB-2026-019: permission prompt back to day 2, plus a pre-permission-explainer arm. Est. 8 weeks.
  • –Android — do not ship. Null result; revisit after the iOS fix settles the prompt question (Android shares the flow).

The redesign works mechanically — users reach a first lesson 38 seconds sooner (CI [−45, −31]) — so ship it where it wins, fix the prompt where it hurts, wait where it’s null.
