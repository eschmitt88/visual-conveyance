Pathways didn’t win overall — it split.

Six weeks, 184,204 new signups randomized 50/50 between the current onboarding (A) and the redesigned “Pathways” flow (B); 13,249 trial starters analyzed. Blended trial-to-paid conversion moved 11.4% → 12.2% — +0.8 percentage points (pp), which is not statistically significant (p = 0.16). The honest overall verdict is “can’t tell”, and the blend hides the real story: a strong mobile-web win cancelling against a real iOS loss. The call: ship mobile-web only, fix iOS, ship nothing else.

The platform stories

Mobile-web — +4.2pp, the unambiguous win

Result

13.9% → 18.1% (+4.2pp, CI [+2.1, +6.3], p < 0.0001; n 2,341 / 2,367; conversions 325 → 428). A ~30% relative lift, significant at any reasonable threshold, and it easily survives the multiple-comparison correction.

Durability

In the late window (weeks 4–6, after novelty washed out) mobile-web still shows +3.8pp (CI ≈ [+0.8, +6.8]) — the gain persists; it is not just novelty.

Why it’s believable

Mobile-web converts through a web checkout with no app-store friction, and it’s where the old flow was clumsiest: the 9-screen flow was designed for native and ported to web. Step 3 there is a soft email opt-in with no OS dialog — drop-off barely moved (3.2% → 3.4%).

Decision

Ship, target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks to confirm the effect out-of-experiment and monitor post-novelty drift.

iOS — −1.9pp, a credible regression with a likely mechanism

Result

11.6% → 9.7% (−1.9pp, CI [−3.6, −0.2], p = 0.027; n 2,528 / 2,541; conversions 293 → 246). A ~16% relative loss; the CI excludes zero. In the late window the deficit stays at −2.2pp (CI ≈ [−4.6, +0.2]) — it does not recover.

How much to trust it

Honestly mixed: at the Bonferroni-adjusted bar (α = 0.0167) for three platform tests, p = 0.027 does not survive the strict correction. Cutting the other way: platform was the single pre-registered subgroup dimension, chosen in advance for a stated mechanistic reason (different payment surfaces) — not found by scanning cuts. Pre-registration plus a coherent mechanism makes this a credible signal that warrants action, while stopping short of proof.

The likely mechanism — the step-3 permission prompt

Pathways moved the notification-permission ask from a day-2 soft in-product card to onboarding step 3, where iOS fires the system permission dialog. Drop-off at exactly that step, on exactly the highest-friction platform (funnel data — exploratory, but directly on the hypothesized mechanism):

Decision

Don’t ship. Fix and re-test: ONB-2026-019 (draft) runs Pathways-on-iOS with the prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus an arm with a pre-permission explainer screen. Same primary metric; powered for a ±1.5pp iOS-only effect; est. 8 weeks.

Android — +0.1pp, null

Result

7.8% → 7.9% (+0.1pp, CI [−1.7, +1.9], p = 0.92; n 1,733 / 1,739; conversions 135 → 137). No evidence of an effect in either direction. Android’s OS permission dialog is lower-friction than iOS’s; step-3 drop-off moved only 5.0% → 5.6%.

Decision

Don’t ship. Revisit after the iOS fix experiment (ONB-2026-019) settles the prompt question, since Android shares the flow.

Before you quote this readout

What Pathways (B) actually changed

The current flow (A) has 9 screens with the notification-permission ask deferred to day 2. Pathways (B) shortens the flow to 6 screens, adds a goal-setting quiz that personalizes the starter course, moves the notification-permission ask to step 3, and adds a social-proof screen. Hypothesis: a faster, more personally relevant first session raises trial-to-paid conversion.

Primary metric: among users who start a free trial, the share whose first payment succeeds within 14 days of trial start (denominator = trial starters, not all randomized users — see “Was the experiment healthy?” for the check that this comparison is fair).

Was the experiment healthy? (SRM, instrumentation, denominator)

Randomization: 184,204 signups split 50/50 at account creation (A 92,114 / B 92,090); SRM check χ² p = 0.96 — no mismatch; weekly monitoring never alarmed. Assignment sticky per account; no crossover observed. Duration was fixed at six weeks in advance; interim looks were monitoring-only, no peeking-based early stop.

Instrumentation: verified against the payments ledger on a 2% audit sample (n = 3,684): zero cross-arm contamination, zero duplicate assignment, conversions reconciled within 0.1%.

Denominator integrity: because the metric conditions on trial start, a treatment that changed who starts trials would bias the comparison. Trial-start rate is statistically flat (7.17% vs 7.22%, p = 0.69) and platform mix among trialists is near-identical across arms (within 0.4pp per platform) — the conditioned comparison is sound.

One deviation from plan: a planned supplementary Bayesian shrinkage estimate of weekly deltas was dropped for time, replaced by the pooled late-window checks. No registered primary or subgroup analysis was altered.

Multiple comparisons & statistical power

Multiple comparisons: three platform tests were run, so the Bonferroni-adjusted threshold is α = 0.0167. Mobile-web (p < 0.0001) survives easily; iOS (p = 0.027) does not survive the strict correction. Cutting the other way: platform was the single pre-registered subgroup dimension, chosen in advance for a stated mechanistic reason (different payment surfaces), not discovered by scanning many cuts — which makes the iOS regression a credible signal that warrants a targeted follow-up, while stopping short of proof. The mobile-web result needs no such hedging.

Power: the pre-experiment analysis gave 80% power to detect a +1.55pp lift overall (11.5% assumed baseline, ~6,400 trial starters per arm, two-sided α = 0.05). The experiment was not powered to confirm effects smaller than ~1.5pp overall — relevant context for the +0.8pp point estimate.

Guardrails: retention flat, a support-ticket blip that resolved

7-day retention is flat: 31.2% (A) vs 31.4% (B), +0.2pp, CI [−0.2, +0.6], measured on all 184k randomized users. Pathways neither helps nor harms whether users come back in week one: the conversion story does not come at a retention cost — and equally, the mobile-web conversion gain is not accompanied by a retention gain.

Support tickets rose +12% in weeks 1–2 (4.1 → 4.6 per 1k signups, driven by confusion around the goal-quiz “skip” affordance per ticket tagging). A copy fix shipped 2026-05-15 to both arms’ shared components and volume normalized from week 3 on (4.0 vs 4.0). Treated as a launch-quality blip, not a standing cost of the design.

Secondary metrics: the flow works mechanically, but that didn’t become a blended win

Median time-to-first-lesson fell 38 seconds: 4m 52s (292s) → 4m 14s (254s), CI [−45, −31], p < 0.001 — the one clearly significant secondary. The shorter flow does what it was designed to do mechanically; it just didn’t translate into a blended conversion or retention win.

Trial-start rate: 7.17% vs 7.22% (+0.05pp, CI [−0.19, +0.29], p = 0.69) — flat. Lessons completed in first 7 days: 3.9 vs 4.0 (+0.1, CI [−0.1, +0.3], p = 0.24) — flat.

Next steps

  1. Ship Pathways to mobile-web only, target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks to confirm the +4.2pp effect out-of-experiment and monitor for post-novelty drift.
  2. Do not ship iOS. Launch ONB-2026-019 (draft): Pathways-on-iOS with the permission prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus a second arm with a pre-permission explainer screen. Primary metric unchanged; powered for a ±1.5pp iOS-only effect; est. 8 weeks.
  3. Do not ship Android. Null result; revisit after the iOS fix experiment settles the prompt question, since Android shares the flow.
  4. Retire the week-2 interim readout (+2.2pp blended, nominally significant at the time) from internal decks; this readout supersedes it.
  5. Re-estimate revenue impact for the mobile-web ship using the late-window +3.8pp (conservative) to full-run +4.2pp range.
