Why the headline “+0.8pp” must not drive the decision

Two independent reasons:

  • It is not significant. p = 0.16 and the CI spans −0.3pp to +1.9pp — it includes “B slightly hurts.” (It equally includes +1.9pp, so “no effect” is also not the honest reading. The blended effect is simply too small relative to noise.)
  • It is not one effect. The blend averages a strong win with a real loss. Weighted by trial-start counts, the platform effects reconcile exactly to the headline:
(2,354 × 4.2  −  2,535 × 1.9  +  1,736 × 0.1) ÷ 6,625 ≈ +0.8pp

The overall number is not “a modest win everywhere” — it is a strong win and a real loss averaging each other out. A global ship decision isn’t supported either way.

When you looked changed what you saw — novelty decay

Consistent with a novelty effect…

The redesign outperformed most among the earliest cohorts and converged toward control as the run matured. A mid-flight readout at the end of week 2 (blended +2.2pp, nominally significant at the time) circulated internally — this final readout supersedes it, and it should be retired from internal decks.

…but the platform split is not novelty

Pooled late-window checks (weeks 4–6, exploratory):

  • Mobile-web still +3.8pp (CI ≈ [+0.8, +6.8]) — the gain persists after novelty washes out.
  • iOS still −2.2pp (CI ≈ [−4.6, +0.2]; no longer individually significant at this halved sample) — the deficit does not recover.

What went wrong on iOS: the step-3 permission prompt

Pathways moved the notification-permission ask from a soft in-product card on day 2 to onboarding step 3 — where iOS fires the operating system’s permission dialog (the one-shot system pop-up asking to allow notifications). Funnel data — exploratory, but directly on the hypothesized mechanism — shows the regression concentrated at exactly that step, on exactly the platform with the highest-friction dialog:

Supporting (but confounded) detail

iOS users who dismissed the step-3 dialog in B converted at 7.9% vs 10.4% for those who accepted — consistent with the prompt souring or filtering the marginal user. This comparison is confounded by user intent and is not causal evidence on its own.

Why “fix and re-test”, not “abandon iOS”

The regression concentrates at exactly the changed step, on exactly the platform where that step is most costly. Follow-up experiment ONB-2026-019 (draft): Pathways-on-iOS with the prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus a second arm with a pre-permission explainer screen. Same primary metric; powered for a ±1.5pp iOS-only effect; est. 8 weeks.

How much to trust this readout

Subgroups: handle with care — but these earn it

Three platform tests were run, so multiple comparisons apply. Under a Bonferroni-adjusted threshold (α = 0.0167): mobile-web (p < 0.0001) survives easily; iOS (p = 0.027) does not survive the strict correction. Cutting the other way: platform was the single pre-registered subgroup dimension, chosen before launch for a stated mechanistic reason (web checkout vs App Store vs Play Store) — not found by scanning many cuts. Pre-registration plus a coherent mechanism makes the iOS regression a credible signal that warrants action — a targeted follow-up — while stopping short of proof. The mobile-web result needs no such hedging.

Power: the overall test couldn’t see +0.8pp

The experiment had 80% power to detect an absolute lift of +1.55pp overall (α = 0.05, ~6,400 trial starters per arm). It was not powered to confirm effects smaller than ~1.5pp — relevant context for the +0.8pp point estimate: an effect that size was never going to read as significant here.

One deviation from plan: a supplementary Bayesian shrinkage estimate of weekly deltas was dropped for time, replaced by the pooled late-window checks. No registered primary or subgroup analysis was altered.

Validity checks: all clean

  • No sample-ratio mismatch: 92,114 vs 92,090 against 50/50; χ² p = 0.96. Weekly monitoring never alarmed.
  • Instrumentation audited: 2% sample (n = 3,684) against the payments ledger — zero cross-arm contamination, conversions reconciled within 0.1%.
  • Denominator sound: conversion conditions on trial start, but trial-start rate is flat between arms (p = 0.69) and platform mix among trialists matches within 0.4pp — so B didn’t change who starts trials.
  • No peeking: 6 weeks fixed in advance; interim looks were monitoring-only.

What this readout does not support

  • Not “B increased conversion by 0.8 points” — the blended effect is not significant, and the blend averages a win with a loss.
  • Not a platform-wide ship — iOS would likely pay a real conversion cost (best estimate −1.9pp on the highest-revenue platform).
  • Not “the redesign failed” — mobile-web is a large, durable, pre-registered, mechanism-consistent win.
  • Not the early-week numbers — the +2.9pp week-1 snapshot reflects novelty that fully decayed and should not be cited.

Recommendation and next steps

  1. Ship Pathways to mobile-web only, target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks to confirm the +4.2pp effect out-of-experiment and monitor for post-novelty drift.
  2. Do not ship iOS. Launch follow-up experiment ONB-2026-019: Pathways-on-iOS with the permission prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus an arm with a pre-permission explainer screen. Powered for ±1.5pp iOS-only; est. 8 weeks.
  3. Do not ship Android. Null result; revisit after the iOS fix experiment settles the prompt question, since Android shares the flow.
  4. Retire the week-2 interim readout from internal decks; this document supersedes it.
  5. Re-estimate revenue impact for the mobile-web ship using the late-window +3.8pp (conservative) to full-run +4.2pp range.
