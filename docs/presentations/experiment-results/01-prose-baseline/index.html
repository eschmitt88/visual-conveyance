Pathways onboarding A/B test: don’t read the overall number as a win

tessel · experiment ONB-2026-014 · final readout, 6-week run (May 4 – Jun 14, 2026), readout 2026-06-30 · pre-registered plan v1.2 followed

We tested the redesigned onboarding flow “Pathways” (B) against the current flow (A) across all new signups for six weeks. Trial-to-paid conversion moved from 11.4% to 12.2% — +0.8 percentage points (pp), 95% confidence interval [-0.3, +1.9], p = 0.16 — which is not statistically significant: the honest overall verdict is “can’t tell,” not “B won.” The real result is a pre-registered platform split — mobile-web improved strongly (+4.2pp) while iOS significantly regressed (-1.9pp) — so the recommendation is to ship Pathways to mobile-web only, hold it off iOS pending a fix experiment on the step-3 notification prompt, and leave Android (a null result) unchanged. Early weeks overstated the effect (+2.9pp in week 1, +0.1pp by week 6, a novelty pattern), but the mobile-web gain persists after novelty washes out. The 7-day-retention guardrail was flat, so the split is not hiding a retention cost.

The overall number: inconclusive, and internally misleading

The blended result cannot support a ship decision in either direction. The +0.8pp point estimate comes with a 95% CI of [-0.3, +1.9] and p = 0.16 (two-proportion z-test, two-sided). A confidence interval is the range of true effects consistent with the data; this one straddles zero, so the data are compatible with B being slightly harmful, neutral, or helpful overall. It should not be quoted as a win — and, equally, not as “no effect.”

Two things make the blended number worse than merely noisy. First, the experiment had 80% power to detect an overall lift of +1.55pp; it simply was not sized to confirm effects around +0.8pp. Second, the overall figure is an average of opposing subgroup effects — a strong win and a real loss partially cancelling — not “a modest win everywhere.” That is the next section.

The real story: the platform split

Platform was the single pre-registered subgroup dimension (chosen before launch because the three platforms have different payment surfaces: web checkout vs App Store vs Play Store), and it is where the experiment’s actionable signal lives.

  • iOS is a significant regression: −1.9pp on an 11.6% base, roughly a 16% relative loss, with a CI entirely below zero.
  • Android is null: +0.1pp with a CI spanning [−1.7, +1.9] — no evidence of an effect either way.

These subgroup effects, weighted by trial-start counts, reconcile arithmetically with the blended +0.8pp. The overall number is a win and a loss averaging each other out.

How much to trust the split. Three platform tests were run, so a multiple-comparisons caution applies: under a Bonferroni-adjusted threshold (α = 0.0167, a stricter bar used when several tests are run at once), mobile-web survives easily, but iOS (p = 0.027) does not survive the strict correction. Cutting the other way: platform was pre-registered in advance for a stated mechanistic reason, not discovered by scanning many cuts, and the iOS regression has a coherent mechanism (below). Read the iOS result as a credible signal warranting a follow-up experiment — not as proof. The mobile-web result needs no such hedging.

Novelty decay: early snapshots overstated B

The overall weekly delta fell from +2.9pp in week 1 to +0.1pp in week 6 (weeks 1–6: +2.9, +1.5, +0.6, 0.0, −0.4, +0.1), a pattern consistent with a novelty effect: the redesigned flow did best with the earliest cohorts and converged toward control as the run matured. Weekly CIs are wide (≈±2.7pp), so the signal is the decaying pattern, not any single week.

A mid-flight readout at the end of week 2 (blended +2.2pp, nominally significant at the time) circulated internally. It reflected novelty that later decayed; this final readout supersedes it, and the week-2 numbers should be retired from decks.

The platform split is not explained by novelty. In exploratory pooled checks over weeks 4–6 (after the novelty had washed out), mobile-web remains +3.8pp (CI ≈ [+0.8, +6.8]) while iOS remains negative at −2.2pp (CI ≈ [−4.6, +0.2] — no longer individually significant at this halved sample size). The mobile-web gain persists; the iOS deficit does not recover.

Guardrails and secondaries

7-day retention was flat: 31.2% (A) vs 31.4% (B), +0.2pp, CI [−0.2, +0.6], measured on all 184,204 randomized users. Pathways neither helps nor harms whether users come back in week one — the conversion story carries no retention cost, and equally the mobile-web gain brings no retention gain.

Support tickets rose +12% in weeks 1–2 (4.1 → 4.6 per 1k signups, driven by confusion around the goal-quiz “skip” affordance); a copy fix shipped 2026-05-15 and volume normalized from week 3 (4.0 vs 4.0) — a launch-quality blip, not a standing cost. Among secondaries, median time-to-first-lesson improved by 38 seconds (4m 52s → 4m 14s, CI [−45, −31]) — the flow mechanically does what it was designed to do — but this did not translate into a blended conversion or retention win. Trial-start rate was flat between arms (7.17% vs 7.22%, p = 0.69), which matters because the primary metric conditions on starting a trial: a flat start rate (and near-identical platform mix among trialists) means the comparison is sound. Diagnostics were clean: no sample-ratio mismatch (p = 0.96) and a 2% instrumentation audit found zero contamination.

Why iOS likely regressed: the step-3 permission prompt

Pathways moved the notification-permission ask from a soft in-product card on day 2 to onboarding step 3, where iOS fires the operating system’s permission dialog. The funnel evidence (exploratory, but aimed directly at this mechanism) concentrates the damage at exactly that step, on exactly the platform with the highest-friction dialog:

  • iOS step-3 drop-off jumped from 8.1% (A) to 12.7% (B) — users abandoning onboarding at the permission step.
  • No comparable shift on mobile-web, where step 3 is a soft email opt-in (3.2% → 3.4%), or on Android, where the OS dialog is lower-friction (5.0% → 5.6%).
  • iOS users in B who dismissed the dialog converted at 7.9% vs 10.4% for those who accepted — consistent with the prompt souring or filtering marginal users, though this comparison is confounded by user intent and is not causal evidence on its own.

This is why the recommendation is “fix and re-test on iOS,” not “abandon iOS.”

Recommendation

  1. Ship Pathways to mobile-web only, target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks to confirm the +4.2pp effect outside the experiment and watch for post-novelty drift.
  2. Do not ship iOS. Run a follow-up experiment (ONB-2026-019, draft) testing Pathways-on-iOS with the permission prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus an arm with a pre-permission explainer screen; powered for a ±1.5pp iOS-only effect, estimated 8 weeks.
  3. Do not ship Android. Null result; revisit after the iOS fix experiment settles the prompt question, since Android shares the flow.
  4. Retire the week-2 interim readout from internal decks.
  5. Re-estimate revenue impact for the mobile-web ship using the late-window +3.8pp (conservative) to full-run +4.2pp range.

In short: not a full rollout, and not a kill. The blended +0.8pp is neither a win nor the story — the split is, and it points to a narrow ship plus a targeted iOS fix.
