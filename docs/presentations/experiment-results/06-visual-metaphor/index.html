The lukewarm river: why the overall number can't decide this ship

Overall trial-to-paid conversion moved 11.4% → 12.2% (+0.8pp, 95% CI [−0.3, +1.9], p = 0.16) — not statistically significant. Do not read this as "B won." The blended number is lukewarm river water: a genuinely warm tributary (mobile-web, +4.2pp) and a genuinely cold one (iOS, −1.9pp) mixing into an average no one actually swims in — so the ship decision has to be made tributary by tributary. (pp = percentage points; a 95% CI is the range of effects consistent with the data — if it includes 0, the data can't distinguish the effect from nothing.)

  • Mobile-web: ship B (with a 5% holdback)
  • iOS: do NOT ship — run a fix experiment on the step-3 permission prompt
  • Android: do NOT ship — null result, revisit after the iOS fix
  • Beware early snapshots: week-1 read +2.9pp — novelty that decayed to +0.1pp by week 6 (the mobile-web gain persisted)

Reading this map

  • The water is people: the 13,249 new signups who started a free trial during the six-week run, flowing from signup toward a first payment (the metric: share of trial starters whose first payment succeeds within 14 days).
  • Temperature is the effect: how B's conversion compares to A's on that platform. Warm = better, cold = worse, gray = the confidence interval straddles zero, so the data can't tell.
  • Color is earned, not decorative: a channel gets solid color only when its 95% CI excludes zero. Everything hatched gray — including the entire blended river — is statistically indistinguishable from "no effect."
  • Width is sample size: wider channels carry more trial starters, and the mouth gauge averages the tributaries weighted by width — which is exactly why +4.2 and −1.9 net out near +0.8.
  • Platform was the one pre-registered subgroup (declared 2026-04-28, before launch, because the three platforms pay through different surfaces: web checkout vs App Store vs Play Store). These tributaries were mapped before anyone saw water — not fished out of the data afterward.
  • The decision is made tributary by tributary, never at the mouth.

At the mouth Why +0.8pp must not be quoted as a win

The gauge at the mouth reads +0.8pp with a 95% CI of [−0.3, +1.9] and p = 0.16. The CI includes zero and effects as negative as −0.3pp, so the correct overall verdict is inconclusive — but it also includes +1.9pp, so "no effect" is equally wrong. Two more reasons the mouth can't carry the decision:

  • It's a mix, not a measurement of anything real. Weighting the tributary effects by their trial counts reconciles to the blended number ((2,354 × 4.2 − 2,535 × 1.9 + 1,736 × 0.1) / 6,625 ≈ +0.8pp). The overall figure is not "a modest win everywhere" — it is a strong win and a real loss averaging each other out.
  • The gauge wasn't built to read this fine. The experiment had 80% power to detect an overall lift of +1.55pp; it was not powered to confirm effects smaller than ~1.5pp. A +0.8pp blip is below what this instrument can resolve. (Power = the chance the experiment would detect an effect of a given size if it were real.)

The warm tributary Mobile-web: a large, unambiguous improvement

+4.2pp on a 13.9% base is a ~30% relative lift (CI [+2.1, +6.3], p < 0.0001) — significant at any reasonable threshold, and it survives the multiple-comparison correction (Bonferroni-adjusted α = 0.0167 for the three platform tests). It also makes mechanical sense: mobile-web converts through a web checkout with no app-store friction, and it is where the old flow was clumsiest — the 9-screen flow was designed for native and ported to web. Pathways cuts it to 6 screens. Crucially, this warmth is not novelty: pooled over weeks 4–6 the mobile-web effect is still +3.8pp (CI ≈ [+0.8, +6.8]).

The cold tributary and its rapids iOS: a significant regression, with a suspect

−1.9pp on an 11.6% base is a ~16% relative loss (CI [−3.6, −0.2], p = 0.027). Honest caveat first: under the strict Bonferroni correction (α = 0.0167), p = 0.027 does not survive. Cutting the other way: platform was the single pre-registered subgroup dimension, chosen in advance for a stated mechanistic reason. Pre-registration plus a coherent mechanism makes this a credible signal that warrants action — a targeted follow-up experiment — while stopping short of proof.

The rapids mark the suspected mechanism. Pathways moved the notification-permission ask from a day-2 soft in-product card to onboarding step 3, where iOS fires the system permission dialog. Funnel evidence (exploratory, but aimed directly at the hypothesized mechanism):

  • iOS step-3 drop-off jumped from 8.1% (A) to 12.7% (B) — of iOS users reaching step 3, A: 31,240 reached / 2,530 dropped; B: 31,020 reached / 3,940 dropped.
  • No comparable shift where the dialog is soft or gentler: mobile-web 3.2% → 3.4% (step 3 is an email opt-in, no OS dialog); Android 5.0% → 5.6% (lower-friction dialog). The regression concentrates at exactly the changed step, on exactly the highest-friction platform.
  • iOS users in B who dismissed the dialog converted at 7.9% vs 10.4% for those who accepted — consistent with the prompt souring or filtering marginal users, though this comparison is confounded by user intent and is not causal evidence on its own.

This is why the plan is "fix and re-test on iOS," not "abandon iOS."

The still tributary Android: no evidence either way

+0.1pp with a CI spanning [−1.7, +1.9] (p = 0.92): null. Android shares the flow with iOS, so it stays unshipped until the iOS fix experiment settles the permission-prompt question.

The season log Early readings ran warm — novelty, decaying

Gauge readings of the blended river, week by week. The overall effect decayed from +2.9pp in week 1 to +0.1pp in week 6 — consistent with a novelty effect. Any decision made on a week-1 or week-2 snapshot would have materially overstated B: a mid-flight readout at the end of week 2 (blended +2.2pp, nominally significant at the time) circulated internally, and this final readout supersedes it.

But the platform split is not explained by novelty. Pooled over the late window (weeks 4–6, exploratory): mobile-web remains +3.8pp (CI ≈ [+0.8, +6.8]) — still warm after the novelty washed out — while iOS remains negative at −2.2pp (CI ≈ [−4.6, +0.2], no longer individually significant at this halved sample). The warm tributary is durable; the cold one does not recover.

The levee and the other gauges Guardrails held; the plumbing is sound

  • 7-day retention is flat: 31.2% (A) vs 31.4% (B), +0.2pp, CI [−0.2, +0.6], measured on all 184,204 randomized users. The conversion story does not come at a retention cost — and equally, the mobile-web conversion gain is not accompanied by a retention gain.
  • Support tickets rose +12% in weeks 1–2 (4.1 → 4.6 per 1k signups, driven by confusion around the goal-quiz "skip" affordance). A copy fix shipped 2026-05-15 and volume normalized from week 3 on (4.0 vs 4.0) — a launch-quality blip, not a standing cost.
  • Time-to-first-lesson improved by 38 seconds (median 4m 52s → 4m 14s, CI [−45, −31], p < 0.001) — the one clearly significant secondary. The shorter flow does what it was designed to do mechanically, but that speed did not translate into a blended conversion or retention win (lessons completed in the first 7 days: 3.9 vs 4.0, CI [−0.1, +0.3], p = 0.24 — flat).
  • The channels weren't rigged: randomization split cleanly (92,114 vs 92,090; χ² p = 0.96, no sample-ratio mismatch); a 2% instrumentation audit (n = 3,684) found zero cross-arm contamination and conversions reconciled within 0.1%. And because the metric conditions on starting a trial, it matters that trial-start rate is flat between arms (7.17% vs 7.22%, p = 0.69) with platform mix among trialists within 0.4pp per platform — so comparing conversion among trial starters is sound.
  • One deviation from the pre-registered plan: a supplementary Bayesian shrinkage estimate of weekly deltas was dropped for time, replaced by the pooled late-window checks above. No registered primary or subgroup analysis was altered.

Where the metaphor breaks

Rivers actually merge; these platforms never do. Downstream of a real confluence there is water everyone shares — here, every user stands in exactly one tributary from signup to payment, and no one ever experiences the "+0.8pp blend." The mouth gauge is an artifact of averaging, not a place. If the map tempts you to ask "but how is the river doing overall?", that is the metaphor misleading you: there is no overall river, only three separate streams and a decision to make on each. Likewise, real water temperature is a physical fact; these "temperatures" are noisy statistical estimates — which is why uncertainty is painted on as hatching that no real river would carry.

The engineering plan Recommendation: a narrow ship, not a rollout and not a kill

  1. Ship Pathways to mobile-web only, target 2026-07-06, with a 5% holdback for 8 weeks to confirm the +4.2pp effect out-of-experiment and monitor for post-novelty drift. (Holdback = a small slice kept on the old flow as an ongoing control.)
  2. Do not ship iOS. Launch a follow-up experiment (ONB-2026-019, draft) testing Pathways-on-iOS with the permission prompt reverted to the day-2 soft ask, plus a second arm with a pre-permission explainer screen. Same primary metric; powered for a ±1.5pp iOS-only effect; est. 8 weeks.
  3. Do not ship Android. Null result; revisit after the iOS fix experiment settles the prompt question, since Android shares the flow.
  4. Retire the week-2 interim readout from internal decks; this readout supersedes it.
  5. Re-estimate revenue impact for the mobile-web ship using the late-window +3.8pp (conservative) to full-run +4.2pp range.
