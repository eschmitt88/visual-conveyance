Diffusion models: destroy carefully, learn to rebuild

How Stable-Diffusion-class image generators turn pure random noise into brand-new images — and why the variant zoo (DDIM, ODE solvers, latent diffusion, CFG, flow matching, distillation) mostly exists to answer one question: how do we take fewer steps?

fixed forward noising learned denoising network sampling cost & speed prompt guidance

space = play / pause · ← → = step

  1. A fixed forward process gradually noises a real image — after T = 1000 small steps, nothing survives.
  2. A network learns to undo one small step; chaining them turns pure random noise into a brand-new image.
  3. Naive DDPM sampling: ~1000 sequential network passes per image — the cost every variant attacks.
  4. DDIM, ODE solvers, rectified flow and distillation cut steps from 1000 toward 1-4, trading some quality.
  5. Latent diffusion runs everything in VAE-compressed space — roughly 48× fewer numbers — with a slight compression ceiling.
  6. Guidance runs the network twice per step, exaggerating the prompt’s pull; s ≈ 7.5 trades diversity for adherence.
  7. One framework, many samplers: generation always starts from pure noise, and no training images are stored.
