Diffusion models & their variants — a guided tour

Every AI-generated image starts as pure random noise

A trained network reverses gradual destruction — no photo edited, no database searched (image-to-image modes, where you supply one, are explicit opt-ins). The variants — DDIM, latent diffusion, CFG, flow matching — mostly exist to cut ~1000 steps to 1–4.

02 · The mechanism — forward

First, destroy — carefully

The forward process is fixed — no learning. Gaussian noise is added over ~1000 small steps. Step 200: slightly grainy. Step 600: only coarse shapes. Step 1000: statistically pure static.

A preset noise schedule sets how much each step adds.

03 · The mechanism — the network

The network learns one skill: guess the noise

Removing all the noise in one jump is near-impossible — countless clean images fit a noisy input. Removing a little is tractable. Training: billions of guess-the-added-noise quizzes, scored by mean-squared error. That stability — no adversarial GAN game — is why diffusion won.

04 · The mechanism — generation

Generation runs the destruction backwards

Start from fresh random noise; denoise step by step — blobs, layout, shapes, texture. The image never existed anywhere. Weights encode statistics, not copies; only images repeated many times in training risk rare near-duplication — exceptional, not the mechanism.

05 · The bottleneck

The catch: the walk back is long

~1000

sequential full-network passes per image (naive DDPM sampling)

Training touches one noise level at a time, but sampling walks the whole chain: ~1000 full network passes, each waiting on the last — nothing parallelizes. In 2020 one image took nearly a minute. Every variant below asks: fewer steps, how?

06 · The fixes — smarter sampling

DDIM: same model, 20–50 deterministic steps

The same trained network admits a deterministic sampler that tolerates big jumps: 20–50 steps instead of 1000, with modest quality loss. Fixed starting noise → identical image every time — enabling inversion and interpolation. Your tool's "steps" slider is exactly this tradeoff.

07 · The fixes — one framework

It's all one equation, solved two ways

In the unified 2021 view, noising is a stochastic differential equation; generation just solves it backwards — randomly (DDPM-like) or as a deterministic ODE (DDIM, in essence). Purpose-built ODE solvers (DPM-Solver) reach good samples in ~10–20 steps — no retraining.

08 · The fixes — where it runs

Latent diffusion: shrink first, generate small

A VAE compresses each image ≈48×; diffusion runs entirely in that small latent space; one decode restores full resolution — putting Stable-Diffusion-class models on consumer GPUs, at a slight compression-quality cost. The VAE only compresses; the diffusion model generates.

09 · Steering — classifier-free guidance

CFG: predict twice, exaggerate the difference

guided = unconditional + s × ( conditional − unconditional )

Conditioning alone follows prompts loosely. Each step runs the network twice — with and without the prompt — and exaggerates the difference by scale s (≈7.5 default). Higher s: stronger adherence, less diversity; past ~12–15, oversaturated artifacts. It doubles per-step cost.

10 · The fixes — retraining for speed

Distillation & consistency: 1–4 steps

1–4

steps, after retraining the model to take big jumps

Below the ~10-step floor, retrain for big jumps: progressive distillation matches two teacher steps in one (1000→500→…→4); consistency models send any noisy point straight to its clean endpoint — 1-step generation. Outputs: slightly softer, less varied — the tech behind real-time demos.

11 · The fixes — reformulation

Flow matching: take the straight path

Rectified flow replaces diffusion's curved paths with straight lines: a training point is a linear image/noise blend; the target, their constant difference. Straight paths need fewer steps. It reframes diffusion rather than rivaling it — SD3 and Flux train this way.
