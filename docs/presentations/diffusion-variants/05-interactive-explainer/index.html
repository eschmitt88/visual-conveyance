Diffusion models & their variants

Destroy an image carefully, and you can learn to rebuild one from nothing

Modern image generators — Stable Diffusion, Midjourney-class systems, DALL·E 3, Flux — run a fixed noising process backwards: a trained network removes noise a little at a time, starting from pure randomness. Most of the variant zoo (DDIM, ODE solvers, latent diffusion, distillation, flow matching) exists to make one thing cheaper — sampling; guidance (CFG) exists to make it follow your prompt.

1 · The core idea

Two processes, opposite directions

A diffusion model is defined by two processes. The forward (noising) process is fixed and involves no learning: take a real image, add a small amount of , and repeat — about T = 1000 steps in the original DDPM formulation — following a predefined . Early steps barely change the image; at step 200 it is slightly grainy, at step 600 only coarse shapes survive, and by step 1000 nothing survives — the result is statistically indistinguishable from pure noise.

The reverse (denoising) process is what gets learned. A neural network is trained to undo one small step: given a noisy image and the step number t (the ), estimate a slightly less noisy version. Generation then means: sample fresh pure noise and apply the learned step repeatedly — t = 1000, 999, 998, … down to 0 — exactly the trajectory in the explorer above.

2 · Under the hood

What does the network actually predict?

“The network denoises the image” hides a design choice. Three output parameterizations are common — and they are mathematically interchangeable (simple algebra converts one to another); they differ in training stability and convention.

ε — noise prediction

Predict the noise that was added; subtracting a scaled version of it recovers an estimate of the clean image. The original DDPM choice and still the most common.

Score prediction

Predict the score — the gradient of the log-probability density of noisy images, i.e. the direction in pixel space that makes the image more probable. The predicted noise is just a negatively scaled score: two descriptions of the same quantity.

v — velocity prediction

Predict a defined mixture of the image and the noise (“velocity”). Introduced with progressive distillation; numerically better behaved at very high noise levels and used by several later models.

Whichever target is used, the network sees the noisy image and the timestep t — plus, in text-to-image models, a representation of the prompt (see guidance, below). Architecturally it is typically a in the Stable-Diffusion-1/2 generation, or a in newer systems.

3 · Training

A denoising exercise, repeated billions of times

  1. Draw a real image from the training set.
  2. Draw a random timestep t — anywhere from light to total noise.
  3. Add the corresponding amount of noise in one shot — a closed-form formula gives the correctly noised image for any t directly, no step-by-step simulation.
  4. Ask the network to predict the noise that was added; penalize with a simple between predicted and actual noise.

That is the entire objective (formally a weighted variational bound on the data likelihood, but the practical loss really is “guess the noise, MSE”). Each example exercises one random noise level, so over billions of examples the network learns denoising at every level: global composition at high noise, fine texture at low noise. No adversarial game as in GANs — no generator and discriminator to keep in delicate balance — and that stability is a big reason diffusion displaced GANs for large-scale image generation.

4 · The variant zoo

One long answer to “how do we take fewer steps?”

Training looks at one noise level at a time, but sampling must walk the whole chain — ~1000 sequential forward passes that cannot be parallelized (each depends on the previous one). Even on a fast GPU, a single image took the better part of a minute in 2020. Click a bar (or a tab) for each family’s story.

DDPM — the original~1000 steps

The 2020 formulation the whole family descends from. The sampler injects fresh randomness at every step and visits all ~1000 timesteps. Excellent quality, impractically slow — this is the “original sin” the rest of the zoo exists to fix.

DDIM — same model, fewer & deterministic steps20–50 steps

DDIM (2020) was the first big win, using the same trained network, unchanged. Its sampler is deterministic — fix the initial noise and you get exactly the same image every time — and it tolerates much larger jumps: visit a 20–50-step subsequence of the 1000 timesteps with only a modest quality loss. Determinism has side benefits: you can invert a generated image back to its noise (useful for editing) and smoothly interpolate between two images by interpolating their noise. When an image tool exposes a “steps” slider defaulting to something like 30, it is exposing exactly this tradeoff.

The score-based / SDE view — one lens that unifies everything~10–20 steps

In 2021, score-based generative models were shown to be the same thing as diffusion in a continuous-time limit — now the field’s standard mental model. The forward process is a ; reversing it needs exactly one learned quantity, the score. With it there are two ways back: a reverse-time SDE (random, like DDPM) or a probability flow ODE — fully deterministic, same distribution of images. DDIM is essentially a discretization of this ODE. Once sampling is “just solving an ODE,” the numerical-solver toolbox applies: purpose-built higher-order solvers (DPM-Solver and successors) get good samples in ~10–20 steps with no retraining. This view also explains why so many variants coexist: different discretizations, paths, or parameterizations of the same underlying object.

Flow matching & rectified flow — straighter paths~20–30 steps (fewer viable)

Why follow the curved paths Gaussian noising dictates at all? Flow matching trains the network to predict a velocity field that transports noise to data along prescribed paths; rectified flow picks the simplest ones — straight lines. A training point is just a linear blend of an image and a noise sample; the regression target is the constant velocity (their difference) along that line. Nearly straight paths need far fewer ODE steps, and the recipe drops diffusion-specific scaffolding (noise schedules, timestep-dependent scalings). It is a close sibling of diffusion, not a rival: with Gaussian paths it recovers diffusion’s probability-flow ODE, and score / noise / velocity stay interconvertible. Stable Diffusion 3 and Flux-class models are trained this way, with transformer backbones, in latent space.

Distillation & consistency models — 1–4 steps1–4 steps

Fast samplers plateau around 10–20 steps; going lower means training for big jumps. Progressive distillation trains a student to match in one step what a teacher does in two, then repeats — halving each round (1000 → 500 → … → 4). Consistency models (2023) train the network so any noisy point on a trajectory maps directly to the same clean endpoint, allowing 1-step generation with 2–4 steps as refinement; latent consistency models (LCMs) bring this to Stable-Diffusion-class latents (~4 steps). Adversarial distillation (SDXL-Turbo-style) adds a GAN-like critic to sharpen few-step outputs. The tradeoff is real: distilled few-step models trade some quality and diversity for speed — outputs tend to be slightly softer or less varied than the 20–50-step teacher — and the extra training stage adds pipeline complexity. This is the technology behind “real-time” generation demos that redraw as you type.

Rules of thumb: below ~10 steps, undistilled models degrade visibly; above ~50, returns diminish rapidly. “More steps” refines the same trajectory — it is not a search over more images. And guidance (next sections) doubles the per-step cost.

5 · Latent diffusion

Do it in compressed space

Pixels are expensive: a 512×512 RGB image is 512 × 512 × 3 = 786,432 numbers, and the denoising network must process all of them at every one of dozens-to-hundreds of steps. Latent diffusion (2022) adds a compression stage: a separately trained maps each image into a much smaller latent representation and back. This is the change that made Stable-Diffusion-class models run on consumer GPUs — and why the model is called “latent.”

Diffusion directly on pixels

full resolution throughout

Everything in sections 1–4 as-is: forward noising, training, and sampling all operate on the full pixel grid — the entire cost is paid at full resolution at every step.

Diffusion in the VAE’s latent space

Pure noise, 64×64×4
16,384 numbers — ≈48× fewer → Denoising loop, in latent space
every step ≈48× cheaper → VAE decoder
one single pass → Image, 512×512
full resolution restored

In Stable Diffusion the encoder downsamples 8× in each spatial dimension: 512×512×3 becomes a 64×64 latent with 4 channels. The compression is perceptual, not lossless — the VAE is trained so the reconstruction looks essentially identical, discarding imperceptible detail. The costs are minor: a slight quality ceiling from the compression bottleneck (historically visible in fine text and small faces, much improved in newer VAEs) and one extra decode pass. Note the division of labor: the VAE is only a compressor/decompressor — the diffusion model in the middle is what generates.

6 · Guidance

Classifier-free guidance: steering with the prompt

Text conditioning gets the prompt into the network (as embeddings from a text encoder such as CLIP or T5, attended to at each denoising step), but conditioning alone tends to follow the prompt only loosely. CFG is the fix used by essentially every modern text-to-image system. At training time the prompt is randomly dropped for ~10–20% of examples, so one network learns both prompt-conditioned and unconditional denoising. At sampling time every step runs the network twice — with and without the prompt — and blends the two predictions by extrapolation:

guided = unconditional + s × (conditional − unconditional)

The difference between the two predictions is “the direction the prompt pulls,” and the guidance scale s exaggerates it. Because of the two passes, CFG doubles the network evaluations per step — it matters when counting sampling cost.

7 · Worth unlearning

Common misconceptions

False“It searches a database and collages existing images.”

No training images are stored or consulted at generation time; a few billion parameters trained on billions of images cannot and do not keep copies — the weights encode statistics, and sampling starts from random noise. Nuance: rare near-duplication of training images has been demonstrated, essentially for images repeated many times in the training set — a real but exceptional memorization failure mode, not the mechanism.

False“It denoises a real photo.”

Text-to-image generation starts from pure synthetic noise. Image-to-image and inpainting modes do start from a user-supplied image — by partially noising it and denoising back — but that is an explicit variant, not the default mechanism.

No“The VAE is the generator.”

The VAE only compresses and decompresses. The diffusion model operating in latent space does the generating.

Only up to a point“More steps always means better images.”

Past the workhorse range (~50 steps), extra steps refine imperceptibly — and for distilled models the intended step count is part of the design.

Wrong knob“The guidance scale is a ‘quality’ knob.”

It is an adherence-vs-diversity knob with an artifact regime at high values; “better” depends on what you want.

One framework“Diffusion, DDIM, score-based models, and flow matching compete.”

They are one framework: identical or interconvertible training targets, with different samplers, paths, and parameterizations.

Glossary

Timestep (t)
position along the noising process; high t = more noise.
Noise schedule
the predefined recipe for how much noise each forward step adds.
ε / score / velocity
three interconvertible choices of network output.
Sampler
the algorithm that turns many network predictions into an image (DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver, …).
Latent space
the VAE-compressed representation where latent diffusion operates (e.g. 64×64×4 for a 512×512 image).
CFG scale (s)
strength of classifier-free guidance extrapolation.
Distillation
training a fast student model to imitate a slow teacher.
NFE
number of function evaluations — how many times the network runs during sampling; the honest cost metric (CFG doubles it per step).
