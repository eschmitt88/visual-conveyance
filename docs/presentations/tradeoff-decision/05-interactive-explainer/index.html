Fernwick · Search Platform · decision brief — proposed 2026-07-01

Vector-search engine: Hearth if the infra hire lands by Oct 1 — otherwise Quiver, with an exit plan

Four candidates for semantic product search. Two are eliminated on must-haves — including the one with the best benchmarks. The survivors split the tradeoff: Hearth (self-hosted) wins the cost trajectory and lock-in; Quiver (managed SaaS) wins operational simplicity. Cost ranking inverts with scale: Quiver is cheapest at 1M vectors and most expensive at 10M and 100M. So the recommendation is an IF/THEN, not a winner.

4.1M vectors today, ×2/yr 30% of queries filtered, rising 100 ms p95 budget ≤$2,500/mo ceiling at 10M 85 QPS steady · 120 peak
Primary — Hearth, IF both hold on 2026-10-01

1. Approved platform-infra hire (req PLAT-7) has signed and started by 2026-10-01, and 2. a ≥4-engineer on-call rotation is committed by the Platform team, in writing, by the same date.

Otherwise — Quiver fallback

If either condition fails: adopt Quiver with a mandatory, budgeted exit plan (adapter interface, quarterly export drills, standing ~3 engineer-week migration budget) — not an open-ended commitment.

Standing override — beats both

If filtered queries exceed 60% of traffic, or common facet selectivity drops below ~2%: Quiver regardless — Hearth's filtered recall has no margin there.

Headcount checkpoint 2026-10-01 · full decision review 2027-01-15 (re-check filter-share trend, PLAT-7, Quiver renewal posture, pgnearest v1.0).

Hearth

self-hosted open-source engine

Wins cost trajectory and lock-in. The real price is ops — and the plan leans on a hire that isn't filled yet.

48 ms filtered · 0.90 recall @5% sel. (at the floor) · $1,400/mo @10M · ~0.2 FTE + on-call
  • Latency: 41 ms p95 unfiltered, 48 ms filtered — comfortable under the 100 ms ceiling; projected ~55 ms at 20M on scaled nodes.
  • Recall@10: 0.94 unfiltered; 0.93 at typical filters; 0.90 at 5% selectivity — exactly at the M3 floor; degraded to 0.86 at 1% in stress tests. Passes, with no margin.
  • Ingest: 25,000 vectors/s → full 10M re-index in ~7 min (best of the service-shaped options).
  • Cost: $520/mo 3-node HA floor (covers to ~5M) · $1,400 @10M · $6,800 @100M. Optional vendor support $1,500/mo not included.
  • Ops: ~0.5 FTE first quarter (deploy, tune, runbooks), settling to ~0.2 FTE, plus a ≥4-engineer on-call rotation. Req PLAT-7 would cover it — approved, not yet filled.
  • Residency: in our VPC, EU region — M4 ✓ and in-VPC (N1) ✓.
  • Ecosystem: 4-yr OSS, ~28k stars, quarterly releases; one breaking index-format change (v3→v4, 2025) — major upgrades are migrations. Hybrid keyword scoring: beta plugin.
  • Lock-in: low — open API, data on our disks.
  • Must-haves: all five pass (M3 at the floor).
deep dive Fallback (all M-pass)

Quiver

managed vector-search SaaS

Zero-ops and the best filtered-recall curve. Costs balloon at scale, lock-in is highest, in-VPC never possible.

22 ms filtered · 0.96 recall @5% sel. · $1,900/mo @10M · ~0 FTE
  • Latency: 18 ms p95 unfiltered, 22 ms filtered — native pre-filtering barely flinches.
  • Recall@10: 0.97 unfiltered; 0.96 filtered at 5% selectivity; held 0.95 even at 1% — best filtered-recall curve in the bakeoff.
  • Ingest: 8,000 vectors/s (tier limit) → 10M re-index in ~21 min.
  • Cost: $180/mo @1M · $740 at today's 4.1M · $1,900 @10M · $23,000 @100M (above 50M their pricing forces a dedicated-cluster Enterprise plan). 12-month price lock; renewal not locked.
  • Ops: effectively 0 FTE; vendor on-call; 99.9% SLA ≈ 43 min/month allowed downtime, recourse limited to credits.
  • Residency: vendor EU-west region — M4 ✓. In-VPC deployment is not offered, ever (N1 unsatisfiable).
  • Ecosystem: 6-yr commercial product; polished SDKs; hybrid keyword+vector scoring built in.
  • Lock-in: highest of the four — proprietary query DSL and hybrid API. Bulk JSONL export exists and vectors are reproducible, so exit is feasible but real: ~3 engineer-weeks today, growing with each proprietary feature adopted.
  • Must-haves: all five pass.
deep dive Eliminated — fails M1 + M2

pgnearest

Postgres extension (v0.9)

Rides our existing Postgres fleet — but too slow when filtered and recall below the quality floor.

110 ms filtered ✗ (budget 100) · 0.89 recall ✗ (floor 0.92)
  • Latency: 95 ms p95 unfiltered — 5 ms to spare — but 110 ms filtered fails M1; the query planner's index-scan + filter interplay blows the budget.
  • Recall@10: 0.89 filtered and unfiltered alike — an index-architecture ceiling, not a tuning problem. Fails M2 (≥0.92) and misses M3's 0.90 floor.
  • Ingest: 3,000 vectors/s → ~56 min full re-index.
  • Cost: ~$240/mo marginal @1M · $1,150 @10M · ~$13,500 est. @100M (unvalidated 4-shard fleet; projected latency fails anyway).
  • Ops: lowest of the self-hosted options (~0.1 FTE) — it's just Postgres, which we already run well.
  • Ecosystem: young — v0.9, two years old, effectively one core maintainer. A v1.0 "targeting recall parity" is on the roadmap, undated — a re-benchmark trigger (S5), not something to wait for.
  • Lock-in: minimal. It's SQL.
deep dive Eliminated — fails M3

Braid

embedded ANN library

Fastest and highest recall in the bakeoff — and it doesn't matter: filtered recall collapses, structurally.

12 ms · 0.98 recall — but 0.71 filtered @5% ✗ (floor 0.90)
  • Latency: 12 ms p95 unfiltered — fastest measured (in-process, no network hop); 31 ms with post-filtering.
  • Recall@10: 0.98 unfiltered — best in the bakeoff. But there is no native filtered search: post-hoc filtering drops to 0.94 at 50% selectivity and 0.71 at 5% — far below M3's 0.90 floor. Structural, not tunable: the index doesn't know about metadata.
  • Ingest: 60,000 vectors/s → ~3 min full index build.
  • Cost: cheapest infra at every scale — ~$210/mo @1M · $700 @10M · ~$4,200 est. @100M — but the 100M figure excludes a DIY sharding/replication layer (~2 engineer-quarters) we'd have to build.
  • Ops: highest engineering cost: ~1.5 FTE for two quarters to build serving, snapshots, replication and a filtering layer, then ~0.3 FTE. In-process memory pressure couples index health to search-service health.
  • Ecosystem: 8 years old, battle-tested as a library; everything service-shaped is on us.
  • Lock-in: medium — proprietary index format (rebuildable) + in-process coupling.

"What would change your mind?" — the five flips

S1 Filtered search becomes primary (>~60% of traffic, or facets more selective than ~2%) → flips to Quiver ▾
Hearth's filtered recall sits at or below the floor there (0.90 at 5% selectivity, 0.86 at 1%) while Quiver holds 0.95 even at 1%. This flips the recommendation to Quiver regardless of headcount. Filter share has grown every quarter since faceted search shipped — this is the standing trigger to watch.
S2 The ops headcount doesn't materialize (PLAT-7 unfilled at 2026-10-01) → flips to Quiver ▾
Hearth without PLAT-7 means on-call and tuning land on four product engineers who already own the search stack — converting the cost advantage into unbudgeted attrition risk. Falls back to Quiver with the mandatory exit plan.
S3 Growth accelerates to ×3/yr → strengthens Hearth ▾
100M vectors arrives in ~2.5 years, the labor-adjusted crossover moves under 2 years out, and Quiver's Enterprise-tier pricing dominates the picture.
S4 Quiver renewal repricing → strengthens Hearth ▾
The quote is locked for 12 months only. A 25% renewal increase (their published list moved ~20% last year) moves the labor-adjusted crossover from ~30M to ~20M vectors. This is why the Quiver fallback carries a mandatory exit plan rather than an open-ended commitment.
S5 pgnearest v1.0 ships recall parity → re-benchmark, don't wait ▾
If its new index type actually reaches ≥0.92 recall and fixes filtered latency, the cheapest low-ops option re-enters. Worth a 1-week re-benchmark when v1.0 exists; not worth waiting for on an undated roadmap.

The Quiver fallback comes with teeth

  • All query construction stays behind our internal SearchIndex interface — no Quiver-proprietary DSL outside the adapter.
  • Quarterly export drill: full JSONL export restored into a scratch Hearth cluster, timed and logged.
  • A standing ~3 engineer-week migration budget, re-estimated at each renewal.
  • Re-evaluate at 30M vectors or the first contract renewal, whichever comes first.

Why Hearth first, not the reverse

  • At our growth rate the labor-adjusted crossover (~30M vectors, ~2029) is inside the lifetime of this decision.
  • Quiver lock-in compounds with every proprietary feature adopted.
  • In-VPC residency is plausibly a hard requirement for 2027 enterprise-seller contracts — a door Quiver keeps closed permanently.
  • Quiver is the better engine today on almost every soft criterion; Hearth is the better position to be standing in come 2029. The condition exists because that position is only reachable with the headcount to hold it.
How the numbers were produced — the two-week bakeoff ▾
All four candidates ran 2026-06-08 → 2026-06-19 on one frozen snapshot of 10M product embeddings (sable-embed-v2, 768-dim float32: the 4.1M live corpus + 5.9M synthetic-but-realistic products) — the 12-month scale the decision must hold at. Self-hosted candidates each got an identical allocation of up to 3 nodes (16 vCPU / 64 GB RAM / NVMe); Quiver was measured from inside our VPC via private peering to its EU-west region, with the ~2 ms network hop included. Load: a 48-hour production query log replayed at 120 QPS with the live 30%-filtered mix; latencies are p95 over sustained 1-hour runs after warm-up. Recall@10 vs exact brute-force ground truth on 10,000 sampled queries. Ingest measured as sustained vectors/second including index build. Costs are vendor quotes (Quiver) and our cloud price list (the rest), infra/subscription only — ops labor is shown separately because it dominates the deltas.
