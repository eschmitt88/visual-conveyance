Choosing a vector-search engine: Hearth if the headcount lands by October, Quiver if it doesn't

Mara Okonkwo, tech lead, Search Platform · 2026-07-01 · Decision checkpoint 2026-10-01 · Full review 2027-01-15

Fernwick's semantic product search runs on a prototype index that is now the top reliability risk in the search stack, and we must pick a production vector-search engine this quarter. A two-week bakeoff (2026-06-08 to 2026-06-19) tested four candidates on the same frozen 10M-vector snapshot — our projected 12-month scale. Two failed hard requirements: pgnearest misses the latency ceiling on filtered queries (110 ms p95 vs the 100 ms budget) and the recall floor (0.89 vs 0.92 required); Braid, despite being the fastest and highest-recall engine we measured, collapses to 0.71 filtered recall — far below the 0.90 floor — because its filtering is post-hoc and structural, not tunable. That leaves Quiver (managed SaaS, zero ops, highest lock-in, most expensive at scale) against Hearth (self-hosted open source, cheapest trajectory, real staffing cost), and neither dominates. Recommendation — conditional, not a winner: adopt self-hosted Hearth IF the approved platform-infra hire (PLAT-7) has signed and started by 2026-10-01 AND a ≥4-engineer on-call rotation is committed in writing by the same date; if either fails, adopt Quiver with a mandatory, budgeted exit plan. One standing trigger overrides everything: if filtered queries exceed 60% of traffic or facet selectivity drops below ~2%, the answer becomes Quiver even if the hire lands, because Hearth's filtered recall (0.90, exactly at the floor) has no margin there.

Two options are out on must-haves, not taste

Five must-have requirements framed the bakeoff: p95 latency ≤ 100 ms at 10M vectors including the 30% of queries that carry metadata filters (M1); recall@10 ≥ 0.92 unfiltered (M2); filtered recall@10 ≥ 0.90 down to 5% filter selectivity (M3); EU data residency (M4); and ≤ $2,500/month run cost at the 12-month scale of ~10M vectors (M5, set by the Engineering Director). Miss any one and the option is out, regardless of its other merits.

pgnearest (Postgres extension) fails M1 and M2. The appeal was real — it rides our existing Postgres fleet and DBA practice, queries are plain SQL, and its marginal ops burden (~0.1 FTE) is the lowest of any self-hosted option. But filtered queries hit 110 ms p95, over the 100 ms budget, and recall@10 is 0.89 both filtered and unfiltered — under the 0.92 floor, and under M3's 0.90 as well. The recall limit is the index architecture itself, not a tuning problem. A v1.0 with a new index type is on the public roadmap but undated; it earns a 1-week re-benchmark if it ships (see sensitivity S5), not a wait.

Braid (embedded library) fails M3. It posted the best raw numbers of the bakeoff: 12 ms p95 unfiltered and 0.98 recall@10. None of that gets to matter, because Braid has no native filtered search. Filtering is post-hoc, so recall collapses as filters get selective — 0.94 at 50% selectivity, 0.71 at 5% — and that is structural: the index doesn't know about metadata. Fixing it would mean building an inverted-index layer ourselves, an estimated ~2 engineer-quarters just to attempt the requirement.

This is the answer to "but the library benchmarks best": yes, and it fails a must-have. Raw speed did not decide this; the decision between the survivors hinges on cost and operations.

The real decision: Quiver vs Hearth, a direct trade

Both survivors pass all five must-haves. Between them, no column dominates.

Quiver (managed SaaS) is the better engine today on almost every soft criterion. 18 ms p95 unfiltered, 22 ms filtered; recall@10 of 0.97 unfiltered, 0.96 filtered at 5% selectivity, and still 0.95 at 1% — the best filtered-recall curve in the bakeoff. Full 10M re-index in ~21 minutes. Ops burden is effectively zero FTE, with vendor on-call behind a 99.9% SLA (≈43 minutes/month of allowed downtime). Hybrid keyword+vector scoring is built in. The costs: the highest lock-in of the four — a proprietary query DSL and hybrid-scoring API, with exit estimated at ~3 engineer-weeks today and growing as we adopt more proprietary features — a 12-month price lock with renewal pricing not locked, and in-VPC deployment (running inside our own cloud account) never offered, which matters because in-VPC is plausibly a hard requirement for 2027 enterprise-seller contracts.

Hearth (self-hosted OSS) is comfortably inside every ceiling — 41 ms p95 unfiltered, 48 ms filtered, projected ~55 ms at 20M vectors — with 0.94 unfiltered recall, best-in-class re-index among the service-shaped options (~7 minutes for the full 10M), low lock-in (open API, data on our disks), and in-VPC EU residency. Its price is operational: ~0.5 FTE for the first quarter, settling to ~0.2 FTE steady-state, plus a ≥4-engineer on-call rotation. And one number deserves emphasis:

Hearth passes the filtered-recall must-have with zero margin. It scored 0.90 at 5% selectivity — exactly the M3 floor — and degraded to 0.86 at 1% selectivity in our stress test. Filter share (currently 30% of queries) has grown every quarter since faceted semantic search shipped. This is why the recommendation carries a standing flip trigger, not just a headcount condition.

The cost picture, honestly: the ranking inverts with scale

The catalog holds 4.1M embedded products today and has roughly doubled every year: ~8M by mid-2027, ~16M by mid-2028, 100M on a 4–5 year horizon. Costs at three scales (infrastructure/subscription only):

  • At 1M vectors: Quiver is cheapest at $180/mo; Hearth's minimum 3-node HA cluster is $520/mo.
  • At 10M vectors: the ranking has flipped — Hearth $1,400/mo vs Quiver $1,900/mo (both under the $2,500 ceiling).
  • At 100M vectors: Hearth $6,800/mo vs Quiver $23,000/mo — 3.4× — because above 50M vectors Quiver's pricing forces a dedicated-cluster Enterprise plan.

On infra alone, the crossover is ~3M vectors — already behind us. At our current 4.1M, Quiver's quote is $740/mo against Hearth's $520. But infra alone is the wrong comparison: Hearth's ~0.2 FTE steady-state is ≈ $2,800/mo of loaded engineer cost. Counting labor, Quiver stays cheaper in total until roughly 30M vectors — around 2029 at 2×/yr growth. So the answer to "the SaaS is cheaper" is: correct until about 2029, if the labor is truly marginal — and it is not, if PLAT-7 is hired anyway for broader platform duties. The cost argument for Hearth is a bet on our own growth curve: at 2×/yr we reach the labor-adjusted crossover in ~3 years, and every year on Quiver past that point costs more and deepens lock-in.

The recommendation, and exactly what would change it

Primary: adopt Hearth (self-hosted), contingent on all of the following holding at the 2026-10-01 checkpoint:

  1. The approved platform-infra hire (req PLAT-7) has signed and started by 2026-10-01. The req is approved but not yet filled — the whole plan leans on it.
  2. A ≥4-engineer on-call rotation for the search-infra service is committed by the Platform team, in writing, by the same date.

Fallback: if either condition fails, adopt Quiver — with a mandatory exit plan, not an open-ended commitment:

  • All query construction stays behind our internal SearchIndex interface; no Quiver-proprietary DSL outside the adapter.
  • Quarterly export drill: full JSONL export restored into a scratch Hearth cluster, timed and logged.
  • A standing ~3 engineer-week migration budget, re-estimated at each renewal.
  • Re-evaluate at 30M vectors or the first contract renewal, whichever comes first.

Standing flip trigger (overrides both): if filtered queries exceed 60% of traffic, or common facet selectivity drops below ~2%, the recommendation becomes Quiver even if the hire lands. Hearth holds 0.90 filtered recall at 5% selectivity and 0.86 at 1%; Quiver holds 0.95 even at 1%. Filter share is trending up — this is the standing trigger to watch.

Why Hearth first and not the reverse. At our growth rate the labor-adjusted cost crossover (~30M vectors, ~2029) is inside the lifetime of this decision; lock-in cost on Quiver compounds with every proprietary feature we adopt; and in-VPC residency is plausibly a hard requirement for 2027 enterprise contracts — a door Quiver keeps closed permanently. Quiver is the better engine today on almost every soft criterion; Hearth is the better position to be standing in come 2029. The condition exists because that position is only reachable with the headcount to hold it.

Other sensitivities worth naming

  • Growth accelerates to 3×/yr: 100M arrives in ~2.5 years and the labor-adjusted crossover moves under 2 years out — strengthens Hearth.
  • Quiver renewal repricing: our quote is locked for 12 months only; a 25% renewal increase (their published list moved ~20% last year) pulls the labor-adjusted crossover from ~30M to ~20M vectors — strengthens Hearth, and is why the fallback carries a mandatory exit plan.
  • pgnearest v1.0 ships recall parity: if its new index type actually reaches ≥0.92 recall and fixes filtered latency, the cheapest low-ops option re-enters — worth a 1-week re-benchmark when v1.0 exists, not worth waiting for on an undated roadmap.

Residual risks on the survivors

Hearth: the plan leans on an approved-but-unfilled hire; one historical breaking index-format change (v3→v4, 2025, forced a full re-index) means major upgrades must be treated as migrations; filtered recall sits exactly at the 0.90 floor with no margin if facet selectivity tightens.

Quiver: renewal repricing is unlocked after 12 months; lock-in compounds as we adopt proprietary hybrid-scoring features; in-VPC is never possible if enterprise contracts demand it; the 99.9% SLA allows up to ~43 min/month of downtime with no recourse beyond credits.

Bottom line

Two options eliminated by must-haves, two survivors trading cost trajectory against operational burden, and one conditional call: Hearth if PLAT-7 starts and the on-call rotation is committed by 2026-10-01; Quiver with a budgeted exit plan if not; Quiver regardless if filtered traffic passes 60% or selectivity tightens below ~2%. Headcount checkpoint 2026-10-01; full decision review 2027-01-15 (re-check the filter-share trend, PLAT-7 status, Quiver renewal posture, and pgnearest v1.0).
