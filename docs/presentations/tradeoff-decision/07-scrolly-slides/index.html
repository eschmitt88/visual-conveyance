3 / 12 · the field

Four candidates, four architectures

Quiver — managed SaaS

Vendor-operated, usage-priced, EU-west region. The "pay to not think about it" option. 6-year-old commercial product.

Hearth — self-hosted OSS

Clustered service on our nodes. 4 years old, ~28k GitHub stars, quarterly releases; optional $1,500/mo support.

pgnearest — Postgres extension

v0.9; rides the existing Postgres fleet and DBA practice. Queries are plain SQL. Effectively one core maintainer.

Braid — embedded library

ANN library linked into the search service. 8 years old, battle-tested as a library; serving, replication, sharding all DIY.

All four ran a two-week bakeoff (2026-06-08 → 06-19): the same frozen 10M-vector snapshot, a 48-hour production log replayed at 120 QPS with the live 30% filter mix, identical hardware for the self-hosted three.

4 / 12 · the gates

Five must-haves — miss one and you're out

  • M1 p95 latency ≤ 100 ms at 10M vectors — including the 30% filtered traffic
  • M2 recall@10 ≥ 0.92 on unfiltered queries (vs exact brute-force ground truth)
  • M3 filtered recall@10 ≥ 0.90 down to 5% filter selectivity — no silent post-hoc recall drops
  • M4 all product and query data stays in an EU region
  • M5 ≤ $2,500/month run cost at the 12-month scale (~10M vectors)

These are gates, not preferences: an option that misses any one is out regardless of its other merits. Two of the four fell here.

5 / 12 · elimination one

pgnearest: ✗ out on filtered latency and recall

110 ms
filtered p95 — over the 100 ms ceiling under the production mix → fails M1 (unfiltered squeaks by at 95 ms)
0.89
recall@10, filtered and unfiltered alike — below the 0.92 floor → fails M2 (and misses M3's 0.90 by the same number)

SQL filtering is exact, but the planner's index-scan interplay blows the latency budget, and 0.89 recall is the index architecture's ceiling — not a tuning problem. A v1.0 promising recall parity exists only as an undated roadmap.

6 / 12 · elimination two

Braid: the fastest, highest-recall option measured — ✗ still out

Braid recall@10 as filters tighten. Also fastest measured: 12 ms p95 unfiltered, in-process.

Braid has no native filtered search; post-hoc filtering collapses recall as filters tighten. Structural — the index doesn't know about metadata — so its chart-topping speed never gets to matter. Raw benchmarks did not decide this.

10 / 12 · the recommendation

The recommendation is an IF, not a winner

IF — at the 2026-10-01 checkpoint, BOTH hold:

  • the approved platform-infra hire (req PLAT-7) has signed and started
  • a ≥4-engineer on-call rotation is committed by the Platform team, in writing

→ adopt Hearth (self-hosted), the primary recommendation.

ELSE → adopt Quiver — with a mandatory exit plan, not open-ended:

  • all query construction behind the internal SearchIndex interface — no proprietary DSL outside the adapter
  • quarterly export drill: full JSONL restore into a scratch Hearth cluster, timed and logged
  • standing ~3 engineer-week migration budget, re-estimated at each renewal
  • re-evaluate at 30M vectors or first contract renewal, whichever comes first

Why the exit plan: the quote is price-locked 12 months only, and Quiver's list prices moved ~20% last year. A 25% renewal bump pulls the labor-adjusted crossover from ~30M to ~20M vectors.

Hearth's plan leans entirely on headcount: ~0.5 FTE the first quarter, ~0.2 steady-state, plus on-call. Without the hire, that lands on four product engineers who already own search — unbudgeted attrition risk.

11 / 12 · what overrides everything

One standing trigger overrides both branches: the filter mix

If filtered queries exceed ~60% of traffic, or common facet selectivity drops below ~2% → the recommendation becomes Quiver even if the hire lands.

0.90 → 0.86
Hearth filtered recall at 5% → 1% selectivity: at the M3 floor, then below it. No margin.
0.96 → 0.95
Quiver at the same selectivities: the best filtered-recall curve in the bakeoff. Barely flinches.

Hearth has no filtered-recall margin; Quiver holds. Filter share has grown every quarter — this is the standing trigger, re-checked at the 2027-01-15 review alongside PLAT-7, Quiver's renewal posture, and pgnearest v1.0.
