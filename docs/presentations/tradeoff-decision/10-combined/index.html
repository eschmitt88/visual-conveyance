Raw benchmarks did not decide this.

No dominant option — the survivors trade directly

  • Hearth wins: cost trajectory (3.4× cheaper at 100M), low lock-in, in-VPC residency (likely needed for 2027 enterprise contracts — a door Quiver keeps closed permanently).
  • Quiver wins: operational simplicity (~0 vs ~0.2 FTE + on-call), and filtered-recall margin (0.95 at 1% selectivity vs Hearth's 0.86).

The cost ranking inverts with scale

  • Quiver is cheapest at 1M ($180 vs $520/mo) but most expensive at 10M and 100M ($1,900 and $23,000 vs Hearth's $1,400 and $6,800).
  • Infra-only crossover ≈3M vectors — already behind us (today, 4.1M: $740 vs $520).
  • Counting Hearth's ops labor (~0.2 FTE ≈ $2,800/mo), the total-cost crossover sits near ≈30M vectors — roughly 2029 at 2×/yr growth.

The two attacks, pre-answered

"Why not the cheaper SaaS?"

  • It's only cheaper below ≈3M vectors on infrastructure — we're at 4.1M, so on infra alone self-hosting is already cheaper today ($520 vs $740/mo).
  • Labor-adjusted, Quiver stays cheaper until ≈30M vectors (~2029 at our 2×/yr doubling) — but that assumes Hearth's ops labor is truly marginal, which it isn't if PLAT-7 is hired for broader platform duties anyway.
  • Quiver's price lock is 12 months; renewal is not locked (their list moved ~20% last year). A 25% renewal increase pulls the crossover from ≈30M to ≈20M vectors.
  • Every year on Quiver past the crossover costs more and deepens lock-in (~3 engineer-weeks to exit today, growing with each proprietary feature adopted).

"Why not the benchmark winner?"

  • Braid tops the charts — 12 ms p95, 0.98 recall@10, 3-minute index build, cheapest infra at every scale.
  • But it has no native filtered search: post-hoc filtering collapses recall to 0.71 at 5% selectivity, far below M3's 0.90 floor. The index doesn't know about metadata — structural, not tunable.
  • 30% of our queries carry filters, and that share grows every quarter. Attempting M3 would mean building an inverted-index layer (~2 engineer-quarters) plus DIY serving, replication and sharding.
  • Raw speed and recall never got to matter. The decision between the survivors hinges on cost and operations, not benchmarks.

What would change our mind

S1 · Filtered search becomes primary — the standing trigger
Filtered share >~60% of traffic, or facets commonly more selective than ~2% → flips to Quiver regardless of headcount. Filter share is trending up.
S2 · The ops headcount doesn't materialize
Hearth without PLAT-7 lands on-call and tuning on four product engineers who already own the search stack — cost advantage becomes unbudgeted attrition risk. Flips to Quiver (with the exit plan).
S3 · Growth accelerates to 3×/yr
100M arrives in ~2.5 years and the labor-adjusted crossover moves under 2 years out. Strengthens Hearth.
S4 · Quiver renewal repricing
Quote locked 12 months only; a 25% increase moves the crossover to ≈20M vectors. Strengthens Hearth — and is why the Quiver fallback carries a mandatory exit plan.
S5 · pgnearest v1.0 ships recall parity
If its new index type reaches ≥0.92 recall and fixes filtered latency, the cheapest low-ops option re-enters. Worth a 1-week re-benchmark when v1.0 exists; not worth waiting for on an undated roadmap.

Residual risks on the survivors — eyes open

If we go Hearth

  • The whole plan leans on PLAT-7 — approved, not filled.
  • Filtered recall has zero margin: 0.90 at the 0.90 floor, 0.86 at 1% selectivity, if facet selectivity tightens.
  • One historical breaking index-format change (v3→v4, 2025, forced a full re-index): major upgrades must be treated as migrations.
  • Hybrid keyword+vector scoring is only a beta plugin (N3 partial).

If we fall back to Quiver

  • Renewal repricing risk (S4); the 12-month lock expires.
  • Lock-in compounds as proprietary hybrid-scoring features are adopted; exit ≈3 engineer-weeks today and growing.
  • In-VPC never possible — a problem if 2027 enterprise-seller contracts demand it.
  • 99.9% SLA allows ~43 min/month down, with no recourse beyond credits.

Why Hearth-first and not the reverse: at our growth rate the labor-adjusted crossover (~30M, ~2029) is inside the lifetime of this decision; Quiver lock-in compounds with every proprietary feature; and in-VPC residency is plausibly a hard requirement for 2027 enterprise contracts. Quiver is the better engine today on almost every soft criterion; Hearth is the better position to be standing in come 2029 — reachable only with the headcount to hold it.
