Choosing a vector-search engine: no winner — an IF/THEN

Fernwick's semantic product search — 4.1M vectors doubling yearly, 120 QPS peak, 30% of queries filtered — needs a production engine this quarter. Four candidates entered a two-week bakeoff at 10M vectors; two fail must-haves, and the survivors trade cost trajectory against operational burden.

8/8

Otherwise Quiver with a budgeted exit plan; filtered traffic >60% or <2% selectivity flips to Quiver regardless.

  1. Four architectures enter; five must-haves — latency, recall, filtered recall, EU residency, budget — gate the decision.
  2. pgnearest is eliminated: 110 ms filtered fails M1; recall 0.89 fails M2 — an index ceiling, not tuning.
  3. Braid is fastest (12 ms, 0.98 recall) but post-hoc filtering collapses to 0.71 — fails M3. Benchmarks didn't decide.
  4. Survivors trade directly: Quiver wins operational simplicity and filter margin; Hearth wins cost trajectory, lock-in, in-VPC.
  5. Infra costs invert: Quiver cheapest at 1M, 3.4× Hearth's price at 100M; crossover ~3M — already behind us.
  6. Add ~0.2 FTE labor (≈$2,800/mo): Quiver stays cheaper until ~30M vectors — about 2029 at 2×/yr growth.
  7. Hearth is primary only if the PLAT-7 hire starts and ≥4-engineer on-call is committed by 2026-10-01.
  8. Otherwise Quiver with a budgeted exit plan; filtered traffic >60% or <2% selectivity flips to Quiver regardless.
